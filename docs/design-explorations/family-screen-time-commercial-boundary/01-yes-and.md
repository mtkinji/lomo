# Yes-And: Two Independent Reasons To Choose Pro

## Original idea

Let a family complete a useful local Screen Time setup for free, while Pro owns
both genuinely advanced controls and the managed-Household coordination layer.

## Working commercial model

The two axes should remain independent:

| | Local or in-person | Managed through a Kwilt Household |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Basic Apple-like controls** | Free | Pro |
| **Advanced Kwilt controls** | Pro | Pro |

Either of two things therefore makes the experience Pro:

1. the rule depends on value created by Kwilt beyond an Apple-like static
   control; or
2. Kwilt coordinates the rule across people or devices as a managed Household
   service.

## Crisp control vocabulary

### Basic controls

A basic control is static, device-local, and based only on ordinary Screen Time
facts:

- a time window or recurring schedule;
- a daily usage allowance;
- a user-selected set of apps, categories, or websites;
- a plain allow/restrict outcome; and
- local editing, disabling, deletion, release, explanation, and recovery.

Authorization, Apple's picker, native enforcement, shield display, and safe
cleanup are substrate—not paid differentiation. Basic does not mean a demo,
permission-only path, or a deliberately frustrating quota.

### Advanced controls

An advanced control uses policy intelligence or state that Kwilt creates:

- a Kwilt Activity or responsibility must be completed first;
- a Money review or other Kwilt capability supplies condition truth;
- multiple conditions are composed with explicit AND/OR semantics;
- an allowance is earned, carried, replenished, or otherwise derived rather
  than entered as a static daily number;
- the rule adapts to a family-day state, prerequisite-app state, reviewed
  exception, or other Kwilt-maintained fact; or
- Chat proposes or changes the policy through Kwilt's reviewed-operation
  contract.

The strongest paid definition is **a control that depends on Kwilt-maintained
truth or Kwilt-created policy logic**. Merely selecting more apps, adding more
minutes, creating more static schedules, or making a restriction stricter does
not make a control advanced.

### Coordination topology

The managed-Household axis becomes Pro when Kwilt does any of the following:

- binds an authorized device to a named Kwilt dependent;
- gives more than one caregiver scoped authority;
- creates or changes policy remotely from another device;
- delivers policy across devices and records desired-versus-applied receipts;
- routes a child request or temporary caregiver exception;
- preserves managed-device history, replacement, reconciliation, or remote
  recovery; or
- coordinates one agreement across multiple children or devices.

This is paid service value even when the underlying rule is simple, because
Kwilt is maintaining a relationship, authority, delivery, and reliability
system—not merely exposing an Apple control.

## Adjacencies

**Yes, and what if it could make the Free baseline deliberately comparable to
Apple rather than artificially smaller?**

- Serves: `jtbd-trust-this-app-with-my-life`
- Job elevation: Maya can judge Kwilt's fit without encountering a permission
  wall disguised as a product.
- New value: The paid distinction becomes easier to explain to customers and
  App Review.
- Cost delta vs. original: medium
- Anti-pattern check: pass; it avoids arbitrary rule, app, and minute quotas.

**Yes, and what if it could make cross-domain truth the signature meaning of
"advanced"?**

- Serves: `jtbd-put-intention-before-impulse`
- Job elevation: The phone responds to what the family actually agreed matters,
  not only to elapsed clock time.
- New value: Activities, Money, Focus, and future Kwilt capabilities can supply
  explicit rule conditions without turning Screen Time itself into the product
  being sold.
- Cost delta vs. original: medium
- Anti-pattern check: pass if conditions remain deterministic and legible; fail
  if AI silently decides whether access is deserved.

**Yes, and what if a basic rule could become worth paying for because it is
remotely coordinated?**

- Serves: `jtbd-invite-the-right-people-in`
- Job elevation: Either caregiver can maintain a simple family agreement
  without becoming the permanent device administrator.
- New value: Pro does not require increasingly complicated rules to remain
  valuable; simple policy gains value through authority and delivery.
- Cost delta vs. original: medium
- Anti-pattern check: pass if roles are explicit and privacy remains scoped.

**Yes, and what if a sophisticated personal rule could be Pro without involving
a family at all?**

- Serves: `jtbd-put-intention-before-impulse`
- Job elevation: Adults and responsible teens can connect access to meaningful
  Kwilt state without needing a Household construct.
- New value: The two-axis model supports advanced personal controls and basic
  managed family controls with one commercial vocabulary.
- Cost delta vs. original: low
- Anti-pattern check: pass if the restriction remains self-authored and
  reversible.

**Yes, and what if downgrade changed management state rather than hiding or
trapping controls?**

- Serves: `jtbd-trust-this-app-with-my-life`
- Job elevation: A former subscriber can always understand and safely remove
  what affects a device.
- New value: Pro creation, remote changes, automation, and delivery can pause
  while reading, loosening, disabling, release, and cleanup remain available.
- Cost delta vs. original: low
- Anti-pattern check: pass; no safety hostage or surprise reactivation.

**Yes, and what if customer copy named the paid service rather than the Apple
mechanism?**

- Serves: `jtbd-trust-this-app-with-my-life`
- Job elevation: People understand why they are paying and what control they
  retain.
- New value: "Coordinate family agreements" and "connect access to what matters
  in Kwilt" replace claims that sound like paying to unlock Screen Time.
- Cost delta vs. original: low
- Anti-pattern check: pass; pricing stays honest and outcome-led.

## Boundary cautions

- A two-condition rule made only from time and usage is technically composed,
  but it is the weakest example to market as paid value because both inputs
  closely resemble Apple's native controls.
- "Strict mode," extra blocked apps, more schedules, or longer enforcement are
  poor Pro boundaries. They mainly change the amount or force of the Apple-backed
  behavior.
- Family Sharing subscription eligibility is separate from Kwilt Household
  membership and separate again from Apple Screen Time authorization.
- App Review discretion remains. The release should preserve a remotely
  controlled fallback that can make advanced Screen Time authoring Free and
  remove it from paid-benefit copy without disturbing Money or the rest of Pro.

## Frame recommendation

**Run the design-thinking loop with the original frame, clarified by the
two-axis model.** The frame is not broader; it is now precise enough to produce
distinct packaging alternatives. The next phase should vary where the local
Free-to-Pro line sits while holding managed Household coordination as Pro.
