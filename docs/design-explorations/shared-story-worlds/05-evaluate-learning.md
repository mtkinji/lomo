# Evaluate Learning: Shared Story Worlds

## Learning questions

1. Can a family understand Goal, Promise, Trouble, commitments, Power, and Keepsake through play rather than explanation?
2. Does physical reveal plus host recording feel energetic or clerical?
3. Do deterministic costs feel earned and fair?
4. Do AI callbacks materially increase delight, ownership, or rematch intent?
5. Does the game remain worth replaying when offline content is used?
6. Are three scenes enough for an emotional arc without overstaying the room's attention?

## Supporting evidence

- Players independently hold up a choice without the host restating the rules after scene one.
- Every seat has at least one visibly consequential commitment.
- Someone proposes using or saving a Power or Keepsake without an app prompt being explained.
- The host records choices without conversation stopping for more than a few seconds.
- Players refer back to a generated callback, laugh at it, or retell it after the ending.
- The group selects **New adventure** without prompting.
- Offline play reaches a coherent ending with no error recovery language.

## Disconfirming evidence

- Players watch the narration instead of discussing choices.
- The host effectively decides for multiple seats or complains about data entry.
- Trouble changes feel arbitrary or the group cannot explain why it changed.
- Players forget their resources, spend them accidentally, or cannot tell whether they mattered.
- AI text is longer than the group's conversation, arrives after the relevant moment, or changes visibly while being read.
- The offline version feels like a broken or obviously lesser game.
- Heroic failure feels punitive to a child.

## Instrumentation

Record only event-level session evidence:

- Adventure started and completed.
- Story flavor and connected/included content source.
- Scene completion duration.
- Commitment distribution counts, not spoken explanations.
- Power and Keepsake use.
- Final outcome.
- New-adventure selection.
- Generation success, invalid response, timeout, or offline fallback.

Do not record generated story text, player dialogue, character names, or individual psychological interpretations for analytics.

For initial family sessions, use a short manual scorecard: explanation needed, host-recording friction, biggest laugh/surprise, resource discussion, ending comprehension, and whether anyone asked to play again.

## Decision rule

- **Proceed:** two mixed-age groups complete, both understand Trouble by scene two, host recording does not stall the room, and at least one group voluntarily replays or retells a callback.
- **Revise:** the story is appealing but recording or resource comprehension interrupts play. Simplify commitments or collapse one resource before adding devices or campaigns.
- **Move AI upstream:** the mechanics replay well but live callbacks do not affect delight or behavior. Use AI to author bundled packs rather than at runtime.
- **Retire the concept:** groups experience it primarily as reading generated text and do not discuss, coordinate, or replay.

The decision follows two complete family sessions and at least one forced-offline session on a signed TestFlight build.
