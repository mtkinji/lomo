## Synthesis: what the current concept is missing

The Progress-versus-Trouble frame is a good dramatic container, but **Careful versus Bold cannot be the entire game**. Repeating the same risk choice would quickly expose generated story as surface variation over a shallow mechanism.

The more compelling version needs five additional properties:

1. **A promise as well as a goal.** The group chooses what it refuses to lose: leave nobody behind, protect the creature, preserve the village, or bring the artifact home intact. The goal says what success means; the promise creates the cost that makes choices emotional.
2. **Three different set pieces.** Each act introduces a small scenario-specific cooperative problem using the same character resources in a new way. One may require choosing a route, another splitting scarce protection, and the finale combining abilities in an order that matters.
3. **Deterministic character agency.** A player's stated action always changes the world. Randomness determines the world's complication or cost, not whether the AI considers the action clever enough to work.
4. **Simultaneous commitments.** At key moments, every player privately chooses Help, Guard, Risk, or Hold on their phone, then the group reveals together. This removes downtime, creates surprise, and makes it harder for one player to command everybody.
5. **A returned consequence.** The twist and finale must reuse a named person, object, promise, or decision from earlier play. This is what makes AI responsiveness emotionally noticeable.

## Sharper proposed game loop

1. **Choose the promise.** The group sees one objective and chooses one of two things it vows to protect.
2. **Meet the characters.** Each person accepts a name, trait, one guaranteed power, and one treasured item. One pet may join with one rescue.
3. **Set piece one: learn.** The group solves a forgiving cooperative problem and sees how its powers combine.
4. **The world pushes back.** A visible threat result advances Trouble and returns something from the players' actions.
5. **Set piece two: divide.** Everyone commits a response simultaneously. The reveal determines which cost the group faces, preventing one player from playing all seats.
6. **The twist.** The AI changes the meaning of the objective without erasing prior progress.
7. **Finale: combine.** Every player contributes part of the plan; remaining abilities, items, relationships, and the pet rescue determine the options available.
8. **Resolve goal and promise separately.** Completing both is a bright victory; completing only the goal is a costly victory; losing the goal but keeping the promise is heroic failure; losing both is a true defeat with a bounded consequence.
9. **Return the hook.** The ending names two or three concrete acts of cooperation and offers one consequence to continue or a clean new adventure.

This moves the product from “take turns describing actions and roll” toward **a compact cooperative scenario generator in which the family's own choices become the components**.

## Mechanics to borrow and reject

Borrow:

- Pandemic's external threat escalation and interdependent roles.
- The Crew's short changing missions and anti-quarterbacking constraints.
- Gloomhaven's exhausted, asymmetric powers and consequential timing.
- EXIT's bespoke reveals and satisfying one-session arc.
- Wildermyth's character-specific callbacks and sideways transformation.
- Jackbox's shared-screen anticipation and simultaneous phone reveal.

Reject:

- D&D's setup, rulebooks, statistics, and required GM skill.
- Gloomhaven's campaign administration and optimization burden.
- Pandemic's ability for one expert to direct every open-information move.
- EXIT's disposable one-play content model.
- App-assisted games' long narration and screen-centered attention.
- Progression that makes returning characters or older pets mechanically stronger than newcomers.
