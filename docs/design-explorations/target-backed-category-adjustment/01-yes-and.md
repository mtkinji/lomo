# Yes-And: Target-Backed Category Adjustment

## Expansion
Yes, and the flow can replace the false question “What is the right number?” with two honest guideposts:

- **What spending suggests:** a recent completed-month average or supported recurring amount, with its evidence period.
- **What the plan can hold:** the largest amount that fits before another category must move, when that value is knowable.

Yes, and after the user proposes an amount, the review can answer the whole decision in one sentence:

- `Planned budgets use 70% of monthly income. $120 comes from unused room and $80 moves from two flexible budgets.`
- `Planned budgets use 72% of monthly income, 2 points above your 70% target.`

Yes, and the review can explicitly preserve the actual-spend truth:

- `This changes your monthly plan. You have still spent $327 in Shopping this month.`

Yes, and `Why $200?` can reveal `From recent spending`, `Fixed bill`, `Set by you`, or `Current exposure` without opening a finance lesson.

## Job elevation
The bigger job is not editing a category. It is reconciling three truths at the moment of correction: what happened, what a credible future amount is, and whether the whole plan still expresses the user's income target.

## Frame recommendation
Run the loop with the expanded frame. The smallest strong version is a guided category adjustment, not a raw input and not a global planner.
