# Yes-And: Budget-Led Quiet Compass

## Original idea

Lead first-install onboarding with one clear offer: Kwilt helps people build a useful budget and
can pause selected spending apps until they review the relevant budget, while other starter paths
remain available with less visual weight.

## Adjacencies

### Yes, and what if it could make Kwilt's differentiation understandable before setup?

- Serves: `jtbd-review-budget-reality-before-spending`
- Job elevation: Move the promise from "another budgeting app" to budget truth at the actual
  spending moment.
- New value: The lead offer explains why Money and Screen Time belong together without presenting
  a bundle of product features.
- Cost delta vs. original: low
- Anti-pattern check: pass, provided the promise remains concrete and does not imply that a shield
  or budget already exists.

### Yes, and what if it could let Maya start with budget clarity even when she does not want app
controls yet?

- Serves: `jtbd-review-budget-reality-before-spending`
- Job elevation: Preserve the core Money job without making device restriction the price of entry.
- New value: Money remains useful for someone who wants the answer but not the intervention.
- Cost delta vs. original: low
- Anti-pattern check: pass. Requiring Screen Time permission during onboarding would be forced
  commitment; offer app controls after the budget promise is understood.

### Yes, and what if it could turn app-control setup into an earned second step rather than a
permission wall?

- Serves: `jtbd-put-intention-before-impulse`
- Job elevation: Ask for Screen Time access only when Maya has chosen a budget and understands what
  the pause will accomplish.
- New value: Permission has an immediate, user-authored purpose and is easier to trust or decline.
- Cost delta vs. original: medium
- Anti-pattern check: pass when decline keeps Money usable and setup can resume later.

### Yes, and what if it could define activation as one trusted decision instead of onboarding
completion?

- Serves: `jtbd-carry-intentions-into-action`
- Job elevation: Measure whether the household plan affected a real choice.
- New value: Kwilt can distinguish route arrival, account connection, budget setup, and a real
  budget-aware decision instead of collapsing them into one completion metric.
- Cost delta vs. original: medium
- Anti-pattern check: pass. Do not gamify decisions or pressure users to trigger a shield merely to
  complete onboarding.

### Yes, and what if the first successful budget check could introduce the rest of Kwilt at the
moment breadth becomes credible?

- Serves: `jtbd-trust-this-app-with-my-life`
- Job elevation: Let demonstrated usefulness earn permission to explain adjacent capabilities.
- New value: Goals, Meals, and other paths can appear contextually after first value rather than
  competing for attention on the opening screen.
- Cost delta vs. original: medium
- Anti-pattern check: pass only if the introduction is quiet, dismissible, and unrelated to
  unlocking core Money value.

### Yes, and what if acquisition language and first install used the same concrete promise?

- Serves: `jtbd-review-budget-reality-before-spending`
- Job elevation: Preserve intent from the promise that motivated installation through the first
  useful action.
- New value: App Store, landing-page, referral, and onboarding language can reinforce one wedge
  even when deferred attribution is unavailable.
- Cost delta vs. original: low
- Anti-pattern check: pass. Unknown intent remains honest; marketing consistency must not be
  treated as proof that every installer wants Money.

### Yes, and what if a later household version could make the budget pause shared without becoming
surveillance?

- Serves: `jtbd-invite-the-right-people-in`
- Job elevation: Carry a household agreement into spending moments while preserving each person's
  authority and privacy.
- New value: The wedge could eventually extend from self-authored controls to explicit household
  coordination.
- Cost delta vs. original: high
- Anti-pattern check: deferred. Current family/child enrollment and delivery scores are too weak;
  adding this to the lead promise now would overclaim and risk parental-control framing.

## Frame recommendation

**Run the design-thinking loop with the original frame.**

The lead-offer concept is already the right-sized product frame. Its most important elevation is
not another feature: budget clarity must work without Screen Time, while budget-aware app controls
become the differentiated optional second step. Activation should be a trusted Money decision or
a completed budget-aware review, not permission grant or setup completion.

For the first-install mockup, include only:

1. One concrete lead promise.
2. One primary action into Money.
3. A clear but secondary set of other starter paths that remain visible and directly tappable on
   the same screen.
4. No permission, account-link, rule-builder, or household setup on the chooser itself.

Defer contextual cross-capability education, acquisition-system work, and household controls until
the core lead offer has earned first-value evidence.
