# Diverge: Chore Recurrence Resolution

Axis: how an unfinished recurring need crosses its next recurrence boundary.

## One universal refresh rule

Every unfinished scheduled occurrence becomes missed when the next valid window arrives, and one fresh occurrence replaces it. This is excellent for time-bound routines but wrong for work whose need persists.

Best when every chore is like feeding a pet or putting bins out. Fails when “clean the bathroom” should remain visible until done.

## One universal persistent rule

Every unfinished occurrence remains open and the next is scheduled only after completion. This prevents duplicates but turns calendar routines into stale work and loses the distinction between yesterday's miss and today's responsibility.

Best when every chore is condition-based. Fails for daily or time-sensitive routines.

## Two explicit outcomes

Repeating chores default to **Start fresh next time** and may instead use **Keep open until done**. Both keep at most one actionable child row. A refreshed occurrence records the old occurrence as missed; a persistent occurrence remains the same fact until completion.

Best when the household mixes calendar routines with persistent maintenance. It adds one progressive choice to caregiver editing but does not add child-facing configuration.

## Separate owed copies

Every scheduled miss remains actionable while new copies continue to arrive. This preserves literal per-occurrence debt but creates duplicate titles, ambiguous completion, and shame-prone backlog behavior.

Best only for a narrow externally consequential obligation. It fails the calm, finite child-list requirement and is excluded from this release.

## Correcting work that was done but not recorded

### Keep missed rows visible

Leave old rows in the child's list with a correction action. This is discoverable, but turns history back into a visible backlog and makes a miss look like debt.

### Caregiver-only history correction

Let a caregiver find a missed occurrence in series history and mark it complete. This preserves authority, but makes the caregiver maintain a ledger and requires a new history surface.

### Child request from the current chore

When the current chore has eligible missed occurrences from this week, reveal one quiet correction entrance in its existing detail drawer. One eligible yesterday uses **I did this yesterday**; several eligible dates use **I did this on another day**. The child may select several real missed dates, while the caregiver resolves each dated occurrence through the existing review queue.

This keeps correction contextual, preserves child agency, and does not make missed history part of the everyday list.
