# Converge: Earned catalog

## Choice

Choose the earned catalog. It improves the weakest common transition—starting—with minimal blast radius and refuses to imply that automated coverage proves fun.

Within that catalog, mature Clue Circle as a timed rapid circle rather than preserving its current changing-clue puzzle. Correct and pass must both advance immediately; the handoff teaches the physical controls without adding a per-turn clue mode; every finder receives the same amount of time; individual results roll into a cooperative circle total.

## Capability delta

Today, a local group cannot begin most games until names are entered, and production gives thin prompt activities the same promise as complete games.

After this slice, the group can start with neutral seats immediately, see useful time/energy cues, jump directly into a no-setup recommendation, and encounter only release-ready tables in production. Development builds retain a clearly labeled Workshop for continued playtesting.

Still unsupported: automatic claims of fun, public rankings, background engagement loops, and promotion of a Workshop game without human table evidence.

## Reductive decisions

- Enhance the existing shelf and setup; add no destination, onboarding, settings, or account step.
- Keep one canonical catalog with explicit maturity metadata.
- Do not add engagement scores, streaks, or telemetry that watches family conversation.
- Do not delete lower-confidence games; retain them as reversible playtest candidates.
- Do not add deck stores, custom packs, team administration, video capture, or public scores to Clue Circle.
- Replace fixed target counts and mid-target clue-mode changes with a short timer and binary correct/pass rhythm.

## Activation

The shelf itself is the ready moment. A single “Play now” recommendation should launch the instant game, while every local setup accepts neutral player names. Workshop labeling appears only where Andrew is evaluating unfinished tables.

## Bet

We're betting that less setup and fewer, better-promised choices will produce more completed and repeated games than equal billing for every implemented concept. For Clue Circle specifically, we're betting that time pressure plus immediate correct/pass advancement will let the physical gesture create more group energy than changing clue instructions around three fixed targets. If it does not, revisit the gesture teaching or timer before adding content systems.

## Success signal

A first-time group can reach a meaningful first action in under 30 seconds, complete a round without Andrew explaining the interface, and voluntarily choose “again.”
