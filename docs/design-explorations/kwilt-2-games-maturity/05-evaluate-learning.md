# Evaluate Learning: Kwilt 2.0 Games maturity

## Questions

- Can a new group choose and reach the first meaningful action in under 30 seconds?
- Does the table understand the next action without Andrew narrating the UI?
- Which games create visible laughter, suspense, collaboration, or animated disagreement?
- Which games earn an unprompted “again”?
- Does Oddball's single incentive remain legible after one terse teaching screen: match the biggest group and avoid being the sole unique answer?
- Does the public marker feel funny and suspenseful rather than shaming, especially with children?
- Can the host record the largest group and optional sole unique player without draining the reveal's energy?
- Does selecting the scoring group from an empty state prevent the scoring disputes observed in the first table session?
- Does six questions feel complete without overstaying the room's interest?
- Is the spoken countdown clear over the music, with the music supporting rather than competing with the reveal?
- Across 3–8 players, does the marker move often enough to matter without making the result feel arbitrary?
- Does Clue Circle sustain rapid clueing, with correct/pass gestures understood without coaching and no audience member becoming the screen operator?

## Evidence

For each table session, record game, group size/rough ages, time to first action, rules questions, abandonment point, replay request, and one sentence about the room's energy. Do not record conversation content, player answers, or identities.

Disconfirming signals are repeated explanation, one person operating while others disengage, dead time, a child being singled out negatively, early exit, or polite completion without replay interest.

## Decision rule

- Promote a Workshop game after at least three sessions across two groups, with independent starts, completed rounds, and unprompted replay in at least two sessions.
- Revise if the core loop is wanted but instructions, pacing, or ending fail.
- Retire or keep internal if the activity is merely pleasant and does not create a repeatable game loop.

## Instrumentation

Use a small manual playtest ledger. Product analytics may count anonymous game starts, completions, and replay taps later, but this slice must not capture prompts, answers, audio, nearby people, or conversation.

## Expected next action

Run the release-ready set on a real iPhone with at least one family group, then promote, revise, or withhold each table before the Kwilt 2.0 TestFlight candidate.

For Oddball, rerun one 3–4-player table and one 6–8-player table. Keep the revised loop only if both groups finish all six questions without scoring disputes, the host selects rather than corrects the scoring group, the countdown can be seen and heard, the marker produces playful tension without personal discomfort, and at least one group requests another game.

For Clue Circle, first run one complete circle on a physical iPhone. Keep the rapid version only if motion reliably distinguishes correct from pass, the audience resumes clueing immediately after each target, and the group chooses another circle without prompting. A Simulator can prove screens and timer transitions, but not the forehead gesture or table energy.
