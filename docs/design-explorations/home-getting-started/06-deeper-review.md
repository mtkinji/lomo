# Deeper review: from isolated prompts to guided usefulness

September 10, 2026. Developed design recommendations; no app implementation or new runtime evidence. Incorporated into the draft product brief and journey overview.

## Main finding

The earlier concept corrected passive discovery by adding invitations, but still relied on an opaque stream of individual next actions. It did not fully design how users understand the destination, postpone work, bring in another person, find their result again or develop a real shared Home.

## Improvements

| Weakness | Revised design | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One card hides the plan | One recommended action with a short visible sequence of ready/next/parked steps | People can understand the payoff and finite scope without configuring the app |
| Most recent path wins | Prioritize explicit purpose, prerequisites, nearest useful payoff and timely collaboration; recency breaks ties | A bank sync or misplaced setup choice must not stall all usefulness |
| Invitations are generic recruitment | Explain what the recipient can do with a specific authorized result | The invitation gives the second person a reason to participate |
| Sender success stops at sending | Design acceptance, exact destination, first contribution and honest recovery | A family adoption flow must work on both sides |
| “A moment appears” still skips the hard part | Invite a real first shared interaction or reviewed post through an existing owner | The feed grows from actual life and deliberate contributions |
| Later means disappear | Park visibly; distinguish lack of interest and need for help | Interrupted parents can return without being nagged or losing the step |
| Progress is missing configuration | Show what already works, the defined remaining steps and the result's durable location | Success feels concrete and does not make the user a system administrator |
| Feed population defines maturity | Evaluate guidance state independently of feed state | Solo success and active setup alongside a busy feed are both valid |

## Experience standard

At any point the person should be able to answer:
1. What is Kwilt helping me get working?
2. What already works?
3. What do I do next, and what will that make possible?
4. What needs another person, device or service?
5. Can I leave and find this again?

A prompt with a correct destination can still fail if these answers are missing.

## Recommendation vs user control

Kwilt should do the work of recommending a path from known intent and real readiness. Do not ask Maya to design her setup checklist. Show an intelligible recommendation and let her change it. Offer appropriate adjacent benefits, but do not grow the selected path's denominator whenever another capability exists.

When intent is unknown, the existing outcome catalog can supply the choices; don't create an additional required questionnaire. Existing eligibility facts narrow the offered action, not infer private motives or household relationships.

## Two important timing decisions

- Collaboration should be offered when it can improve the outcome. Invite meal input before a shared choice becomes settled when that is the purpose; do not delay a solo grocery payoff merely to recruit another user.
- Device connection does not by itself reveal a desire to restrict a child's apps. First invite the supported participation choices. Specific Screen Time guidance follows that choice or other explicit relevant intent.

## Household adoption proof

Track separate transitions: authorized invitation created, membership accepted, content made available through explicit scope, recipient action, and repeat useful participation. Absence of recipient action does not mean the sender failed setup. Do not expose app-open or read surveillance as onboarding status.

Current source has bounded Household invitation preview/acceptance and some exact Home destinations. That does not establish that a particular meal-plan destination survives installation or auth. The learning release must prove the full handoff or expose honest recovery.

## Persistent Home

Four valid combinations illustrate the design:

| | Feed empty | Real feed content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Useful setup still active | Clear sequence and recommended step | Concise sequence with accessible actual content |
| Chosen outcome ready or guidance minimized | Confirmed useful result and route back; optional discovery | Feed leads; Getting started remains accessible |

No minimum post count, completion streak or global app score chooses between these states. A new user can need deliberate guidance; an established solo user can want quiet utility. The design must support both without requiring a social conversion.

## Learning additions

Ask participants to name the outcome and next two steps, resume a parked step, and find the useful result without the guide. Test the recipient independently, not only the organizer. Check that active setup survives an arriving post and that settled solo use survives an empty feed. These are stronger evidence than clicks on an invitation.
