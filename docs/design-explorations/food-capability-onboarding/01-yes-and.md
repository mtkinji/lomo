# Yes-and: Make Meals Easier

## Expansion

1. **Yes, and one Food door can replace product taxonomy.** Maya chooses the household outcome once;
   Recipes, Meal Planning, and Groceries remain implementation ownership rather than decisions she
   must understand.

2. **Yes, and the first result can match the work that is pressing now.** Someone close to a shop,
   someone deciding dinners, and someone already at the stove should not all be forced through meal
   planning first.

3. **Yes, and saved food knowledge becomes more valuable without becoming the promise.** Recipes
   improve suggestions, ingredient truth, and cooking continuity over time, but an empty Recipe
   library does not block first value.

4. **Yes, and family participation can feel like relief rather than setup.** The guide can show that
   others may weigh in, while the first plan remains complete for one organizer acting alone.

5. **Yes, and automatic ingredients can demonstrate why the capabilities belong together.** The
   selected meals create the reviewed list; Maya does not retype ingredients or wonder which plan
   produced them.

6. **Yes, and Cook Mode can close the promise.** The journey does not stop after planning or
   shopping. Keeping the current cue, quantities, and timer context visible makes the final act of
   getting food onto the table easier too.

7. **Yes, and exact context should shorten the experience.** A shared Recipe, an active Plan, or a
   Grocery continuation can skip the broad Food guide and teach only the missing concept at the
   moment it matters.

## Job elevation

The elevated job is not “organize my recipes” or even “make a meal plan.” It is:

> Help me get from not knowing what we are eating to food on the table with less repeated work.

## Guardrail

Do not convert this expanded promise into a mandatory tour of every Food feature. Breadth should be
communicated once, then the experience should narrow to the person's current need.
