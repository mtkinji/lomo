# Diverge: Food Capability Onboarding

All alternatives begin after the person selects **Make meals easier** from the global chooser. All
use the illustrated-orientation archetype and must end at a capability-owned native result.

## A. The Whole Meal Loop

Show three full-screen illustrated moments before asking where to begin:

1. **Plan meals without carrying every decision.** Show a small flexible meal set with optional
   household input.
2. **Your ingredients come together automatically.** Show chosen meals becoming one reviewed list.
3. **Cook one useful step at a time.** Show a focused Cook Mode cue rather than a long recipe page.

Then ask **What would help right now?** with `Plan a few meals`, `Get the ingredients together`, and
`Make cooking easier`.

Strengths:

- Makes the complete promise unmistakable.
- Gives illustration and brand storytelling a strong role.
- Helps a person understand why three native capabilities belong to one Food experience.

Trade-offs:

- Adds three screens after the universal Welcome and global chooser.
- Teaches moments the person may not currently need.
- Risks feeling like a feature tour before the first useful action.

## B. One Loop, Then the Relevant Guide

Show one full-screen illustration of the connected loop:

> **From figuring out what to make to getting it on the table, Kwilt keeps the work connected.**

Then ask **What would make meals easier right now?**:

- **Plan a few meals**
- **Get the ingredients together**
- **Make cooking easier**

After the choice, show only one or two illustrated moments necessary for that continuation, then
enter the native action. For example, the planning branch can teach flexible planning and optional
family input; the ingredient branch can teach recipe-derived list truth; the cooking branch can
teach resumable one-cue Cook Mode.

Strengths:

- Communicates the broad promise without a mandatory three-feature tour.
- Keeps interaction minimal: one capability-level decision followed by relevant education.
- Supports a clean account and exact-context bypasses.
- Lets each branch earn its own illustration needs and first-value proof.

Trade-offs:

- The single loop illustration must communicate breadth without becoming diagrammatic or busy.
- People may not learn every Food benefit on day one.
- Requires three polished clean-start continuations, not merely one planning funnel.

## C. Plan First, Reveal the Loop Through Results

Show one promise screen, then immediately help the person create a small `Next meals` set. Reveal
the other capabilities only when the plan makes them useful:

- after meals are kept, show the compiled Grocery continuation;
- when a meal is opened to make, introduce Cook Mode; and
- when another household member becomes relevant, introduce participation.

Strengths:

- Fastest route to one durable first result.
- Avoids a second chooser and almost all upfront teaching.
- Makes progressive disclosure do most of the onboarding work.

Trade-offs:

- Treats planning as the universal starting point even when the person came to shop or cook.
- Under-communicates the broader acquisition promise.
- Can make Recipes and Cook Mode feel incidental rather than part of one coherent Food experience.

## Comparative read

| Alternative | Broad promise | Speed to relevant action | Minimal teaching | Supports different current needs | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Whole Meal Loop | Highest | Lowest | Low | High | Becomes a feature tour |
| B. One Loop, Then Relevant Guide | High | High | High | Highest | Demands a very clear first composition |
| C. Plan First | Medium | Highest for planning | Highest | Low | Recreates a planning-shaped funnel |

## Recommended direction for convergence

**B. One Loop, Then the Relevant Guide** best matches the agreed strategy. It makes the Food promise
larger than Recipes or planning, preserves full-screen illustration moments, and avoids making every
person watch the entire capability family before acting.

The recommendation is a bet, not yet convergence: one broad Food illustration plus one simple
current-need choice will provide enough bearings without feeling like another chooser or a tour.
