# Diverge: Three ways to organize shared life

Axis of variation: what organizes the experience—time, relationship/place, or participation. All alternatives include direct posts, capability offers, photos, responses, and chosen audiences. The choice is not between richer and smaller feature lists.

## A. Shared-life stream

One chronological stream brings together posts from your household and approved connections. People lead the cards; attachments add context. A composer is available in Home and the same composition flow opens from capabilities. Household and profile views offer focused browsing and history. Direct arrivals and pending actions remain recognizable above the stream.

- Persona/challenge fit: Maya can catch up in one visit, whether people shared a dinner, a place, or support. David receives only his intended slice.
- System fit: extends the existing feed route, sender presentation, navigation, and delivery patterns; adds durable post ownership, media, conversations, and following.
- Best when: people share asynchronously across capabilities and relationships.
- Fails when: a busy connection overwhelms close household moments or actionable invitations disappear among photos. Remedy with an easy household view and a stable pending-action area; test before introducing ranking.

## B. Household rooms

Home opens onto relationship spaces: your household, another household you follow, and selected people. Each room contains its own stream, photos, invitations, and memories. Capability offers choose a destination room. A small arrival summary helps people notice new contributions across rooms.

- Persona/challenge fit: clear belonging and a strong sense of "our place," particularly for extended family; David can stay in a narrow relationship context.
- System fit: reuses household identity, but adds room navigation and a rule for reconciling exact-person and group audiences. Existing recipient deliveries do not map cleanly to rooms.
- Best when: families strongly distinguish household life from wider connections.
- Fails when: users must check many rooms or post the same moment repeatedly to reach overlapping people. Requires deduplication and cross-room catch-up.

## C. Moments that become shared experiences

Home groups content around ongoing moments: a weekend outing, a project, dinner, or a supported Goal. Photos, notes, and invitations accumulate around the same experience over time. Direct standalone posts remain possible, but related activity is presented as a living story people can join and revisit.

- Persona/challenge fit: Maya sees how sharing can become doing together; a trip can include several people's perspectives. David's support has durable context.
- System fit: strongest potential continuity across Plan, Explore, Games, and Goals, but needs an explicit shared-experience model and participant consent. Temporal proximity cannot silently establish membership or merge stories.
- Best when: groups repeatedly participate in the same outing, project, or event.
- Fails when: everyday posting becomes a filing decision, or the app incorrectly groups separate people's lives. Keep attachment optional and grouping explicit.

## Primer and object-model check

All three preserve private Arcs/Goals/Activities. An optional post is an authored social object, not a new planning unit or a requirement to create an Activity. It can refer to an existing object without sharing that object's private internals. Chapters remain retrospective; none of these concepts uses a Chapter as an upcoming event container.

Capture finishes before any sharing offer. All alternatives reject default-public content, identity scores, streak pressure, compelled posting, and invented AI sentiment. A social feed is an intentional extension requested by Andrew; the old blanket anti-feed language cannot be used to erase that intent. None requires engagement ranking or infinite consumption to work.
