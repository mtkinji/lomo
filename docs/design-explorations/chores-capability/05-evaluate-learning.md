# Evaluate Learning: Digital Chore Rewards

## Learning questions

1. Does a child understand the difference between available, set-aside, and total tokens?
2. Does `Waiting for $N` feel trustworthy rather than as though tokens disappeared?
3. Can a child find and use cancellation when a caregiver has not paid?
4. Does the caregiver understand that **Paid** follows the outside-app transfer and is not approval?
5. Is the separate Rewards attention discoverable without competing with chore review?
6. Does the wallet remain subordinate to the chore inventory?

## Supporting evidence

- Charlie can explain that set-aside tokens are still his and cannot be redeemed twice.
- A pending payout remains visible to both actors until paid or cancelled.
- Caregiver settlement reduces total tokens exactly once and removes attention.
- Rewards-off fixtures contain no token language or wallet action.
- No participant believes Kwilt or Plaid transferred money.

## Disconfirming signals

- A child describes set-aside tokens as lost, spent, or owned by the caregiver.
- Caregivers regularly overlook pending payouts.
- Users expect an accept/reject step before payment.
- The wallet distracts from choosing and completing chores.
- Manual outside-app settlement creates more disputes than clarity.

## Instrumentation

For the learning release, use focused ledger and screen tests plus direct household observation. If analytics are later added, record only Rewards opened, reservation created, reservation cancelled, and payout settled. Do not record token amounts, cash amounts, child performance rankings, or behavioral scoring.

## Decision rule

Proceed toward a permanent capability after repeated household use shows that children can explain the three balances and caregivers settle without prompting from the child. Revise reminders or caregiver entry if payouts linger. Retire redemption while retaining simple earned balances if the manual exchange creates distrust.

## Expected next action

Dogfood the Labs slice with digital-only tokens and a real outside-app payout, then compare the child's explanation before and after one cancellation and one completed settlement.
