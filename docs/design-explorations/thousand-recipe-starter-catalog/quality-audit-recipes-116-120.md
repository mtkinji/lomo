## Identity checks

- Chicken larb is gently water-cooked and dressed off heat with fish sauce, lime, smoky chile, mint, and coarse dark-toasted rice. The note acknowledges its Lao and northeastern Thai/Isan traditions rather than claiming a single point of invention.
- Chicken kathi roll uses yogurt-mustard-oil thigh kebab, a laminated Kolkata-style porota, onion, lime, green chile, and chaat masala. It does not become a generic sauce-heavy chicken wrap.
- Paneer tikka wrap keeps the tikka dry-charred and creamy-centered, then uses thick herb chutney and drained pickled onion to protect a scratch whole-wheat roti.
- Mumbai vada pav includes a crisp gram-flour batata vada, soft ladi pav, green chutney, dry garlic-coconut chutney, and fried chile.
- Pav bhaji remains a plated vegetable bhaji with butter-toasted pav. It is mashed and reduced on a wide pan, not blended into a purée or turned into a sandwich.

## Safety and execution checks

- Ground chicken reaches 165°F before the acidic dressing and delicate herbs are folded in off heat.
- Chicken thigh is refrigerated while marinating and broiled to 165°F; skewers are loaded loosely for even cooking.
- Paneer and vegetables receive a brief high-heat broil so the vegetables blister without making the cheese rubbery.
- Vada frying specifies a deep heavy pot, limited batches, and a 350°F target; wet green chiles are never added to hot oil.
- Pav bhaji vegetables cook fully tender before mashing, while the tomato base is reduced separately to prevent a watery final texture.

## Carry-forward rules

- Preserve traditional cooking behavior even when the roster title frames a dish as handheld; lettuce cups do not justify stir-frying larb.
- Do not convert non-star audience signals into star ratings.
- For fried street food, author the culturally defining dry chutney or condiment rather than hiding it behind a generic purchased sauce.
- Distinguish dishes that share the same bread: vada pav is an assembled sandwich; pav bhaji is a plated mash with bread alongside.
