## Familiarity and effort check

- Psari plaki is the easiest recipe in the group: a familiar 55-minute baked white-fish dinner with one skillet and supermarket ingredients.
- Soutzoukakia is a familiar meatballs-and-rice form with 85 minutes of preparation and cooking plus a short chill. Baking replaces active shallow-frying.
- Spanakopita takes about 1 hour 45 minutes including rest. Frozen spinach and one full pie reduce burden, but phyllo handling keeps it out of quick recommendations.
- Gemista takes more than two hours and depends on good summer produce. It remains a relaxed weekend cuisine anchor despite the familiar stuffed-vegetable form.
- Youvetsi takes about 2 hours 40 minutes because beef must become tender before orzo is added. It is a comforting weekend beef-and-pasta stew, not an everyday default.
- No recipe in this batch is discovery-tier, and none requires restaurant equipment or rare luxury ingredients.

## Safety and execution checks

- Youvetsi beef braises to about 195°F for tenderness and the completed beef-orzo casserole reaches 165°F.
- Gemista filling reaches 165°F and vegetables stay covered until the rice has enough steam to cook safely and evenly.
- Soutzoukakia reaches 160°F during baking and 165°F after simmering in sauce; a pasteurized egg is used.
- Spanakopita uses pasteurized eggs and reaches 160°F in the center.
- Psari plaki reaches 145°F and the vegetable sauce is precooked so fish does not remain in the oven while raw onions soften.
- Every listed ingredient is allocated to preparation, cooking, finishing, or service.
