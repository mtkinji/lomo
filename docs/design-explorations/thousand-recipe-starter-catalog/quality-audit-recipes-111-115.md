## Identity checks

- Bulgogi wraps remain a participatory Korean ssam meal: thin pear-marinated beef, fresh lettuce and perilla, and homemade fermented ssamjang are kept separate until each one-bite parcel is made.
- Ssambap centers multigrain short-grain rice, multiple leaves, and thick vegetable-tofu gang-doenjang. It does not repeat the preceding beef recipe under a different name.
- Scallion beef rolls use scratch laminated scallion pancakes, sliceable braised shank, hoisin, cucumber, cilantro, and scallion. The note identifies their Taiwanese association while retaining the approved broad Chinese roster label.
- Rou jia mo uses chopped spice-braised pork in pan-baked bai ji mo. It is not served in a fluffy steamed bun.
- Gua bao uses the contrasting Taiwanese architecture: folded steamed bread, an intact belly slice, pickled mustard greens, cilantro, and sweet peanut powder.

## Safety and execution checks

- Thin bulgogi is marinated under refrigeration and cooked in uncrowded batches until no raw center remains.
- Cooked rice is shaped warm, then served within two hours or refrigerated promptly.
- Beef shank and both pork-belly preparations simmer until tender; braising liquids are reduced rather than spooned into bread unchecked.
- Raw dough receives full rise/rest time and is cooked through by pan-baking or steaming.
- The gua-bao steamer rests closed for three minutes to prevent collapsed, wrinkled buns and steam burns from an abrupt lid lift.

## Highest-rated pattern synthesis

- Maangchi’s gang-doenjang is the clearest signal at 5.0 from 258 votes: deeply fermented, vegetable-rich sauce makes rice and seasonal leaves a satisfying meal without meat.
- Korean Bapsang’s bulgogi at 4.67 from 191 and Maangchi’s ssamjang at 4.4 from 316 explain the successful wrap as thin tender beef plus a small amount of assertive fermented seasoning—not a wet lettuce taco.
- The Woks of Life’s rou jia mo is 4.91 from 40, while China Sichuan Food’s is 5.0 from 11. Both identify long-braised pork, correct wheat mo, and controlled broth as the quality center.
- Scallion beef roll and gua-bao decisions rely on Taiwanese and Chinese recipe-author authority, especially where English-language star data is sparse.

## Carry-forward rules

- Adjacent roster items must be differentiated by their true center of gravity, not merely by title.
- Author culturally defining wrappers instead of substituting convenient tortillas or generic frozen buns.
- Fermented sauces should be potent and portioned; more sauce is not automatically more authentic or more delicious.
- Audit total rest time across dough, meat, and rice even when some intervals can overlap in real cooking.
