## Identity checks

- The Cuban sandwich explicitly selects Tampa’s documented salami-inclusive lineage, authors its citrus-garlic roast pork, uses Cuban bread, and presses the loaf until thin and crisp. It does not silently collapse Tampa and Miami styles.
- Roasted pork bánh mì follows the Vietnam Tourism Board’s Ho Chi Minh City pattern: airy bread, roast pork, pâté, mayonnaise, cucumber, cilantro, pickled vegetables, and a final savory seasoning drizzle.
- Chicken katsu sando uses evenly pounded chicken, flour-egg-panko breading, homemade tonkatsu sauce, cabbage, and shokupan. Its yōshoku context is acknowledged rather than treating it as a generic fried-chicken sandwich.
- Tamago sando stays intentionally spare: egg, Kewpie, milk, trace sugar, butter, and crustless shokupan. Herbs, mustard, bacon, and American egg-salad crunch are intentionally absent.
- Salmon onigiri uses plain Japanese short-grain rice, warm shaping with wet salted hands, a compact salty salmon center, and nori added only at service. It is not treated as vinegared sushi rice.

## Safety and execution checks

- Pork shoulder cooks to 195–205°F for tenderness; pork loin cooks to 145°F and rests before thin slicing.
- Chicken cutlets reach 165°F, drain on a clean rack, and rest briefly before assembly.
- Eggs are fully cooked, ice-chilled, and mixed only when cold.
- Salmon reaches 145°F and every pin bone is removed before portioning.
- Cooked rice is served within two hours or cooled promptly; the recipe does not imply indefinite room-temperature holding.

## Highest-rated pattern synthesis

- Tamago sando has the strongest direct signal in this group: Just One Cookbook’s Japanese-authored version is 4.72 from 400 votes. Its success comes from restraint, Kewpie, milk, correct salt, butter as a moisture barrier, and pillowy bread.
- Just One Cookbook’s chicken katsu sandwich is 4.91 from 22 votes. The transferable signals are even chicken, dry vegetables, panko texture, shokupan, and tonkatsu sauce; our version fries the cutlet for a more explicit katsu crust.
- Salmon onigiri combines Just One Cookbook’s 4.76 from 239 with Japanese Cooking 101’s small 5.0 signal. Both emphasize correct rice, warm gentle shaping, salty relatively dry salmon, and nori management.
- Cuban sandwich and bánh mì decisions prioritize originating and national cultural sources over generic rating widgets because style boundaries and bread identity are the central questions.

## Carry-forward rules

- When two cities maintain legitimate versions of a named dish, select and label one rather than inventing a false consensus.
- Bread texture can be culturally defining: Cuban bread, Vietnamese baguette, and shokupan are not neutral containers.
- Simplicity may be the highest-quality choice; do not add complexity that erases convenience-food identity.
- Rice handling and holding instructions deserve the same safety scrutiny as meat and eggs.
