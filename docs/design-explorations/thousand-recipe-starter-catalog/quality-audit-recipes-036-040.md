# Quality audit: recipes 036–040

Date: 2026-08-06
Proof state: desk-reviewed; no kitchen-test claim.

## Result

BR036–BR040 pass the editorial contract, unique-body checks, TypeScript, formatting, ingredient allocation, named-component completeness, food-safety review, and identity review.

## Identity checks

- Gyeran bap remains a deliberately simple Korean hot-rice and egg bowl rather than a generic loaded grain bowl.
- Thai jok is fully broken-down and garnished with ginger and white pepper; it is distinct from the visibly grained Japanese okayu and the chicken-based Chinese congee already authored.
- Bánh cuốn identifies its skillet method as a home adaptation rather than claiming equivalence to cloth steaming.
- Bò kho is broth-forward, lemongrass-and-warm-spice aromatic, and presented with baguette without claiming breakfast is its only context.
- Tocino silog authors tocino, sinangag, itlog, and vinegar rather than hiding components behind prepared-food ingredients.

## Safety checks

- Jok requires pasteurized eggs for the traditional soft bowl-set presentation.
- Bánh cuốn filling is fully cooked and dried before rolling.
- Bò kho uses refrigerated marination and a full tenderizing simmer.
- Tocino is labeled refrigerated marinated meat, not shelf-stable cured pork; it is simmered tender and then cooked to 145°F before caramelizing.

## Carry-forward rules

- Similar porridges need grain-state, protein, garnish, and cultural-context distinctions in the method—not just different cuisine labels.
- When adapting specialized equipment, name the adaptation and preserve the sensory target without claiming identical results.
- For sugar-heavy marinades, separate tenderizing from final caramelization so safety and browning do not compete.
