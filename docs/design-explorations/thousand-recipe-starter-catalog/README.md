# 500-Recipe Starter Catalog

Status: 500 recipes authored and integrated after the final desk-review gate;
original 1,000-meal roster retained as an expansion backlog

## Sequence

1. Select exactly 500 distinct meal and recipe concepts from the expansion
   roster, covering every major food category.
2. Review the roster with Andrew before recipe authoring.
3. For each aligned item, compare at least three strong variations when
   available, including one culturally authoritative source for a named
   traditional dish.
4. Record why the strongest versions work and where reviewers report failures.
5. Write an original Kwilt formulation and method from the cross-source
   findings, never by paraphrasing one source.
6. Validate internal math, timing, yield, scaling, ingredient use, equipment,
   allergens, dietary tags, and step order.
7. Publish in reviewable batches with immutable provenance and editorial state.

## Original expansion-roster mix

The following mix describes the retained 1,000-meal expansion backlog. It does
not expand the active 500-recipe completion gate. The final selected tier mix
will be audited directly from the authored 500 recipes before integration.

- **347 cross-cuisine household anchors:** highly recognizable, repeatedly
  useful recipes weighted by broad rating volume and recurring popularity.
- **592 cuisine anchors:** signature regional dishes, many of them everyday
  household favorites within their own communities, that keep global coverage
  from becoming a list of Americanized trends.
- **61 discovery and celebration recipes:** less-frequent projects, seasonal
  food, and dishes that make the catalog feel expansive.

These labels are editorial lenses, not user-visible rankings. `Cuisine anchor`
does not mean obscure; butter chicken, pad thai, pho, and carbonara are cuisine
anchors and widely familiar favorites. Only 61 entries are intentionally
discovery-led.

## Naming and meal-story contract

Job: When a household browses or opens an unfamiliar meal, they need to know
what kind of food it is before deciding whether it fits, while still being able
to learn and use the dish's real name.

- **Primary action:** decide whether to plan or cook the meal.
- **Must show:** a familiar food description in the title, the traditional
  name, the appetite-led description, time, yield, ingredients, and method.
- **Reveal later:** a short `About this meal` passage on the detail page that
  explains origin, meaning, meaningful variation, or the technique that defines
  the dish.
- **Must not add:** academic citations, a separate culture tab, ornamental
  badges, or story copy above the planning and cooking decision.
- **Reuse map:** editorial `notes` supplies the Kwilt-authored meal story;
  personally owned recipes retain the existing `Notes` label for memories,
  variations, and cooking guidance.
- **Required states:** familiar-only names, traditional names with familiar
  clarification, long but readable stories, and personal recipes whose notes
  are not cultural editorial.
- **Proof path:** Recipe detail through Meals, including a Kwilt-authored
  catalog meal and a personal recipe at realistic text sizes.

## Research scorecard for each aligned meal

Ratings alone are insufficient. Each candidate source receives:

| Signal                 | Weight | What it answers                                                  |
| ---------------------- | -----: | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rating confidence      |     20 | Is the score supported by enough reviews to mean anything?       |
| Reviewer evidence      |     20 | What do successful cooks praise and what repeatedly fails?       |
| Technique coherence    |     20 | Do time, temperature, ratios, sequence, and doneness cues agree? |
| Cultural authority     |     15 | Is a traditional claim grounded in a credible regional voice?    |
| Household practicality |     15 | Can a normal home cook source, execute, scale, and recover it?   |
| Distinctive payoff     |     10 | Which choices create the texture and flavor people value?        |

The output is not “copy the winner.” It is a short editorial finding:

- non-negotiable technique;
- flavor structure;
- repeated reviewer success signals;
- repeated failure and substitution signals;
- tradition versus adaptation decision;
- Kwilt formulation rationale;
- sources consulted with access date;
- kitchen-test state: `desk-reviewed`, `cooked-once`, `repeat-validated`.

## Non-negotiable recipe checks

- Every ingredient is used and every step has the ingredients it names.
- Yield and portion assumptions are explicit.
- Timing includes inactive waits and rests separately from active work.
- Doneness uses sensory cues plus safe temperature when relevant.
- Salt is measurable where practical; “to taste” is reserved for finishing.
- Variations never share a generic method when the technique actually differs.
- Traditional names are not used for materially different dishes without an
  `inspired by` disclosure.
- Dietary and allergen facets describe the written recipe, not a vague possible
  substitution.
- No recipe is labeled kitchen-tested until it has actually been cooked.
