- Cornish pasty follows the protected raw beef, potato, swede, and onion composition, D shape, and side crimp while avoiding a false PGI claim outside Cornwall.

## Safety and execution checks

- Cornish pasty beef reaches at least 145°F and vegetables become fully tender within the staged bake.
- Sausage-roll ground pork reaches 160°F; shaped logs are kept cold before baking.
- Ham and cheeses are assembled for immediate service rather than held warm or left out beyond the stated tempering window.
- Uitsmijter specifies pasteurized eggs, fully set whites, and an alternate firm-yolk endpoint for vulnerable diners.

## Highest-rated pattern synthesis

- Cornish pasty evidence comes from a formal protected specification and the Cornish trade association, which outrank generic star ratings for identity.
- Sausage-roll sources show hundreds of recorded ratings but no reliable average on the accessible page, so no score is invented; convergence supports onion, pork, bacon, mustard, flaky pastry, and egg wash.
- The 5/5 uitsmijter scores have only one and four votes. They corroborate the Dutch ham-Gouda-egg structure but do not independently establish superiority.
- Ploughman’s lunch and bacon butty are quality-of-components foods whose most useful evidence explains temperature, cut, texture, and service timing.

## Carry-forward rules

- Protected shape and closure techniques matter alongside ingredients.
- Record small ratings honestly and state their weak evidentiary weight.
- For culturally contested condiments, preserve legitimate diner choice rather than manufacturing one universal answer.
- Cold composed meals still need timing and temperature direction.
