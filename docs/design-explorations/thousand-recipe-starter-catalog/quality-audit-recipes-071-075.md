# Quality audit: recipes 071–075

Date: 2026-08-06
Proof state: desk-reviewed; no kitchen-test claim.

## Result

BR071–BR075 pass batch and global validation, unique-body checks, TypeScript, formatting, ingredient allocation, food-safety review, and cultural-identity review after one contract-only correction: the roster's `D` prioritization label was mapped to the supported `cuisine-anchor` editorial tier for BR073.

## Identity checks

- Ukrainian syrnyky are dry farmer-cheese cakes shaped and exterior-floured individually; they do not duplicate BR068's pourable quark pancake batter.
- Russian buckwheat blini are yeast-raised, egg-white-lightened, and cooked in small rounds for savory toppings.
- Adjarian khachapuri uses a high-sided boat, molten cheese, late-added egg, butter, and table-side mixing. The accessible cheese blend is disclosed rather than called traditional Georgian cheese.
- Israeli bourekas include the complete rostered breakfast plate: feta-potato pastry, hard-boiled egg, chopped salad, tahini, and zhug.
- Colombian calentado is framed as a flexible leftover practice, with cold rice and beans reheated through hogao rather than a freshly cooked rice bowl.

## Safety and execution checks

- Syrnyky centers reach 160°F, and their cheese-moisture check appears before flour quantity is finalized.
- Khachapuri uses pasteurized eggs for soft yolks and adds them only after the cheese and bread are nearly done.
- Bourekas allocate all five eggs: one filling egg, one egg wash, and three hard-boiled eggs.
- Chorizo reaches 160°F and the leftover rice-bean mixture reaches 165°F.

## Carry-forward rules

- Migration histories and cross-border ownership should be named when a nationally cataloged dish has a wider regional lineage.
- Accessibility substitutions for culturally specific cheeses must be explicit and technique-compatible.
- Leftover dishes need both prior-storage assumptions and a reheating temperature; “warm through” is not sufficient.
