## Identity checks

- Soy sauce chicken remains Cantonese see yao gai: a whole bird gently poached and turned in aromatic soy master sauce, not teriyaki or a baked soy-glazed chicken.
- Steamed fish uses the culturally meaningful whole-fish banquet and home-table branch, with cloudy plate liquid removed before the fresh-aromatic hot-oil finish.
- Salt-and-pepper shrimp retains shell-on Cantonese texture and a dry white-pepper finish rather than becoming peeled shrimp in a wet chile sauce.
- Chicken chow mein selects the dry Hong Kong-style pan-fried egg-noodle branch and explicitly separates it from both lo mein and American celery-gravy chow mein.
- Yangzhou fried rice preserves pale separate grains with shrimp, pork, egg, peas, and scallion while disclosing historical and restaurant variability rather than claiming one official formula.

## Safety and execution checks

- Whole chicken reaches 165°F in the breast and at least 175°F in the thigh; serving sauce is separately boiled and master-stock storage requires rapid cooling and freezing.
- Whole fish reaches 145°F and the hot-oil finish is applied from a small pan to a heatproof platter.
- Shrimp are dried before 375°F frying, cooked to 145°F, and frying oil is moved only to a heatproof container.
- Chow mein chicken reaches 165°F and wet noodles are dried before hot-oil pan-crisping.
- Yangzhou shrimp reach 145°F and returned egg reaches 160°F; components are cooked separately to avoid endpoint conflicts.
