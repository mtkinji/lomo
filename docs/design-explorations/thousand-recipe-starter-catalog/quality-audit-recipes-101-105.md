## Identity checks

- The Maine lobster roll is the cold, lightly mayonnaise-dressed style, with large pieces of lobster and a butter-griddled top-split New England roll. It is not the warm Connecticut butter style.
- Pulled pork uses dry-brined shoulder cooked until collagen releases, then gains crisp bark-like edges under the broiler. The recipe is labeled broadly Southern rather than claiming one disputed regional sauce tradition.
- Sloppy Joe preserves its deliberately messy ground-beef, sweet-tangy tomato, and soft-bun identity while browning meat and tomato paste for more depth.
- Nashville hot chicken gets its defining heat from a post-fry cayenne-oil brush and retains soft white bread and cold pickles. Its Black Nashville and Prince family origin is explicitly credited.
- Muffuletta uses the round sesame loaf, mortadella, salami, ham, two cheeses, and a marinated oily olive salad on both bread faces. It is served cold/room-temperature, not transformed into a generic hot Italian sandwich.

## Safety and execution checks

- Cooked lobster stays chilled, is held only briefly, and is mixed without shredding.
- Pork shoulder reaches the tenderness band of 195–205°F, rests before pulling, and is sauced only after cooking.
- Ground beef reaches 160°F and its sauce reduces before it enters the bun.
- Chicken thighs reach 175°F; frying stays at 325°F, and hot oil is handled only after the chicken leaves the fryer.
- Muffuletta rests at cool room temperature for 30 minutes and includes a two-hour refrigeration boundary.

## Highest-rated pattern synthesis

- The largest signal is Allrecipes’ Sloppy Joes at 4.7 from 7,983 ratings. Its durability comes from familiar ingredients and a real simmer; Simply Recipes’ 4.8 from 60 explains the browning technique that improves that base.
- Serious Eats’ pulled pork at 4.9 from 20 provides the key indoor-barbecue insight: keep the meat moist rather than wet and expose part of it to create dark bark.
- Bon Appétit’s Hattie B’s hot chicken at 4.2 from 371 is less universally rated because the dish is intentionally extreme, but its cayenne-oil method is more identity-relevant than maximizing broad palatability.
- Lobster roll and muffuletta decisions rely primarily on Maine, New Orleans, and originating-business authority, where exact construction matters more than generic popularity.

## Carry-forward rules

- When regional styles conflict, name the selected style instead of blending them invisibly.
- Separate sauce from meat until texture is established; wetness is not the same as juiciness.
- Cultural credit belongs in the recipe record when a famous dish emerged from a specific community or business.
- Extreme foods should remain legible to their tradition; rating optimization must not sand off their defining character.
