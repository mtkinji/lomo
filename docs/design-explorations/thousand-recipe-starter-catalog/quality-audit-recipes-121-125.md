## Safety and execution checks

- Dried chickpeas are soaked and cooked fully tender; assembled chaat is served immediately or refrigerated within two hours.
- Samosas use a firm sealed dough and staged 300°F-to-350°F frying in a deep heavy vessel; frying batches stay limited.
- Pork shoulder braises at 325°F until fork-tender, then browns only after the liquid evaporates.
- Whole skirt steak reaches at least 145°F and rests before thin cross-grain slicing.
- Chicken thighs reach 165°F before shredding; the tinga is reduced separately so moist filling does not flood the tostada.

## Carry-forward rules

- A composed street-food recipe must author its defining bases and sauces, not only list assembly shortcuts.
- When regional bread cannot be reproduced faithfully, disclose the substitution and choose it by functional behavior.
- Keep mild cooking sauce and optional high-heat salsa separate when the cuisine does.
- For crisp tostadas and chaats, moisture management is part of the recipe’s identity, not merely serving advice.
