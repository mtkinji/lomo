## Result

BR076–BR080 pass batch and global validation, unique-body checks, TypeScript, formatting, ingredient allocation, food-safety review, and cultural-identity review without post-validation recipe correction.

## Identity checks

- Costa Rican gallo pinto uses day-old white rice, black beans and their liquid, sweet pepper, Salsa Lizano, and cilantro. Its shared Nicaraguan history and Costa Rican regional variation are explicit.
- Dominican mangú remains a hot, silky green-plantain mash with vinegary red onion. Because the roster asks for eggs rather than Los Tres Golpes, the recipe does not imply that an incomplete cheese-and-salami plate is the full traditional combination.
- Jamaican ackee and saltfish retains distinct ackee lobes, properly desalted cod, thyme, scallion, sweet pepper, and restrained Scotch bonnet.
- Haitian spaghetti fully authors its epis and retains hot dogs, ketchup, and tomato paste as meaningful occupation-era and household elements rather than erasing them in pursuit of an Italian pasta ideal.
- Brazilian cuscuz nordestino requires flocão and steaming; it is expressly distinguished from fine-cornmeal porridge, semolina couscous, and São Paulo cuscuz.

## Safety and execution checks

- Both leftover rice dishes specify cold previously cooked rice and a 165°F reheating endpoint.
- Mangú uses room-temperature reserved cooking water while the plantains are hot, preventing the mash from seizing.
- Ackee is commercially canned, with an explicit warning that unripe or incorrectly prepared fresh ackee is toxic. Salt cod is soaked with two water changes and tasted before any salt is considered.
- Fully cooked hot dogs are browned and reheated to 165°F.
- Soft fried and poached eggs are pasteurized.

## Carry-forward rules

- For culturally central convenience products such as Salsa Lizano, canned ackee, or hot dogs, quality means choosing and handling the correct product—not replacing it to appear more artisanal.
- Cross-border dishes require precise regional markers plus an explicit non-exclusive note.
- A safety warning must change the ingredient choice or method; a warning detached from execution is insufficient.
