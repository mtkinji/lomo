## Desirability, familiarity, and effort

- Rosemary focaccia and ciabatta are established favorites with clear sandwich,
  table-bread, and olive-oil uses.

  its distinct grain earns inclusion without adding advanced technique.
- `Rustic Italian country loaf` leads before `Pane casereccio`, and `Golden sesame

## Stories and naming

- Blue corn bread clearly distinguishes a modern Southwestern skillet bread from
  Hopi piki and explains that blue corn is Indigenous maize, not dye.
- Focaccia leads with its familiar rosemary name and explains regional variation.
- Ciabatta translates `slipper` and truthfully identifies its 1980s Veneto origin.
- Pane casereccio is explained as a broad home-style category, not a single
  protected pan-Italian formula.
- Sesame semolina bread explains durum wheat and Sicilian variation while using a
  familiar English catalog title.

## Safety and execution checks

- Hot cast iron, hot Dutch ovens, and boiling-water steam all have explicit handling
  warnings or controlled transfer methods.
- Blue corn and focaccia have center-doneness checks; all yeast loaves reach at
  least 200°F.
- Ciabatta requires face and hands away from steam and removes the steam pan only
  when safe.
- Semolina specifies fine remilled flour to prevent a gritty hydration mismatch.
- Every listed ingredient is allocated to preparation, cooking, finishing, or
  service.

  bake survive.
