# Quality audit: recipes 061–065

Date: 2026-08-06
Proof state: desk-reviewed; no kitchen-test claim.

## Result

BR061–BR065 pass batch and global validation, unique-body checks, TypeScript, formatting, ingredient allocation, food-safety review, and cultural-identity review without post-validation correction.

## Identity checks

- Scottish tattie scones use only cooked, steam-dried potato with minimal flour and are rolled into thin farls.
- Irish boxty combines wrung-dry raw potato with mashed potato and buttermilk, producing a genuinely different griddle cake.
- BR063 is transparently labeled as a composed Scandinavian-style breakfast rather than falsely assigned to one national canon.
- Swedish ugnspannkaka uses a rested, thick milk batter in a rectangular metal pan and is distinguished from thin pannkakor, bacon-filled fläskpannkaka, and the American Dutch baby.
- Finnish riisipuuro begins in water, finishes slowly in milk, and treats the hidden almond as an optional festive tradition.

## Safety and execution checks

- Jammy eggs are pasteurized and timed from gently boiling water.
- Potato moisture is explicitly managed before flour enters either Scottish or Irish batter.
- Milk porridge uses low heat, frequent scraping, and a post-cook consistency adjustment.

## Carry-forward rules

- Similar appearance is not duplication: ingredient state, hydration, cooking vessel, leavening, and cultural role must all be compared.
- Pan-regional labels need explicit transparency when no single national provenance is claimed.
- Starch recipes require moisture-state instructions because ingredient weight alone cannot protect texture.
