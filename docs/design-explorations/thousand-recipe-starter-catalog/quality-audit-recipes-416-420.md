## Familiar naming, stories, and effort

- Both gumbos lead with Louisiana and their primary ingredient identity. Their
  stories explain gumbo's family variation, roux and thickener choices, and why
  the two versions are not interchangeable duplicates.
- Seafood gumbo is the sole special-occasion project: three seafoods, shell
  inspection, shell stock, roux, okra, and precise late cooking are disclosed.
- Chicken-and-sausage gumbo has a long but single-pot process centered on a dark
  roux; it omits okra and seafood to remain focused.
- Classic vegetable minestrone is a flexible everyday pantry-and-produce meal.
- Italian pasta and bean soup leads before `pasta e fagioli`, then explains the
  name and regional variation on the detail page.
- Italian wedding soup keeps its familiar American name while correcting the
  myth: `minestra maritata` refers to the marriage of greens, broth, and meat,
  not a wedding ceremony.
- Three recipes use ordinary ingredients and familiar equipment, satisfying
  the batch gate; none exists merely for cuisine coverage.

## Safety and execution checks

- Both roux instructions require continuous stirring and a full restart after
  scorching. Warm stock is incorporated gradually.
- Chicken thighs reach 175°F before shredding. Filé enters only off heat.
- Seafood is refrigerated until use; crab is checked for shell; shrimp reaches
  145°F, oysters curl, and crab only warms through at the end.
- Minestrone and pasta e fagioli stage vegetables and pasta to protect texture;
  both disclose leftover thickening and a separate-pasta option where useful.
- Wedding-soup meatballs finish at 160°F. Tiny pasta and greens enter late.
- Every listed ingredient is allocated to preparation, cooking, finishing, or
  service.

## Research synthesis

- The gumbo recipes privilege Louisiana food authorities and local New Orleans
  evidence over generic star chasing. Dark roux, trinity, andouille, chicken,
  rice, and off-heat filé define the first; medium roux, okra, shell stock, and
  late seafood define the second.
- Minestrone combines 4.8/5 across 203 ratings and 4.95/5 across 20 reviews with
  a four-recipe comparison. Layered vegetable broth, beans, restrained tomato,
  timed produce, small pasta, herbs, and Parmesan recur.
- Pasta e fagioli combines a 4/5 Italian pattern with two practical home-meal
  sources. Fully softened aromatics, beans, small pasta, modest tomato, garlic,
  herbs, and finishing oil recur.
- Italian wedding soup has exceptional household evidence: 4.92/5 across 279
  reviews, 4.9/5 across 41 reviews, and 4.5/5 across 9,100 ratings. Mini
  meatballs, greens, tiny pasta, rich broth, vegetables, and Parmesan explain
  its broad appeal.
