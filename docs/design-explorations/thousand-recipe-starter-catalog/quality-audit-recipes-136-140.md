## Identity checks

- Chacarero keeps the Chilean defining quartet of thin beef, crisp-tender green beans, tomato, and ají verde, with mayonnaise treated as a restrained contemporary option.
- Chicken döner uses the Turkish home technique of freezing a tightly marinated chicken stack, shaving it thinly, and browning it hard before wrapping it in lavash with sumac onion.
- Chicken shawarma uses a Levantine lemon-garlic-cardamom profile, charred thigh meat, toum-style garlic sauce, and pickles, keeping it clearly distinct from döner.
- Falafel is made from soaked but uncooked dried chickpeas, abundant fresh herbs, rested batter, and controlled deep frying; canned chickpeas are explicitly rejected.
- Sabich is identified as Iraqi Jewish-Israeli and retains fried eggplant, hard-cooked egg, tahini, and amba, with hummus and chopped salad as common street-food additions.

## Safety and execution checks

- Chacarero beef reaches 145°F and rests; blanched beans are shocked and dried.
- Döner chicken is shaved while firm, cooked in uncrowded batches, and reaches 165°F throughout.
- Shawarma thighs reach 165°F before a brief edge-crisping finish; the eggless garlic emulsion is kept chilled.
- Falafel frying is held near 350°F in a deep heavy pan, with dry chickpeas and uncrowded batches reducing splatter and oil-temperature collapse.
- Sabich eggplant is pressed dry before entering 365°F oil; eggs are fully hard-cooked.

## Highest-rated pattern synthesis

- Chicken shawarma draws on a 4.91/5 recipe with 105 ratings and a 5/5 toum recipe with 7,708 ratings. Their shared advantage is technique, not ingredient volume: thigh meat, sustained marination, thin pieces, high heat, edge browning, and a slowly built garlic emulsion.
- Falafel draws on a 4.94/5 Lebanese family recipe with 354 ratings. Its strongest repeated signals—also confirmed by independent test kitchens—are soaked raw chickpeas, thorough drying, abundant herbs, a rested mixture, and properly hot oil.
- The regional Chacarero and döner sources do not expose reliable star-and-count pairs. Their evidence strength comes from cultural proximity and convergence on defining preparation details.
- Sabich has limited numerical review data, so the recipe prioritizes chef, oral-history, and Iraqi Jewish cultural sources rather than inventing a popularity hierarchy.
