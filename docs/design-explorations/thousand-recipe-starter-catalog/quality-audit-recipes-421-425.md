# Quality audit: recipes 421–425

Date: 2026-08-06
Proof state: desk-reviewed; no kitchen-test claim.

## Result

SA001–SA005 pass source comparison, roster continuity, original-body checks,
ingredient allocation, identity review, safety review, familiar-title review,
and the strengthened familiarity/effort gate. The staged corpus now contains
425 of the approved 500 recipes; the live catalog remains untouched.

All five are household anchors. The full mix is now 167 household anchors, 223
cuisine anchors, and 35 discovery recipes.

## Desirability, naming, and stories

- Every title is a familiar menu name: Caesar, Cobb, chef, chicken ranch, and
  Buffalo chicken. No parenthetical translation or specialist knowledge is
  needed to decide whether the meal sounds appealing.
- Caesar's story accurately locates its creation in Tijuana and explains that
  it was named for Caesar Cardini, not Julius Caesar. The cuisine label avoids
  silently recasting that history as solely American.
- Cobb's story explains its Brown Derby identity and why composed rows protect
  texture and choice.
- Chef salad is explicitly differentiated from Cobb through its turkey, ham,
  two-cheese, cucumber, egg, and deli-counter profile.
- Chicken ranch leaves out Cobb's bacon and avocado, using cabbage, corn,
  cheddar, and pepitas for a more everyday chopped identity.
- Buffalo chicken preserves the Buffalo, New York wing logic—hot sauce, butter,
  celery, and blue cheese—without requiring breading or frying.

## Familiarity and effort check

- All five use ordinary supermarket ingredients and standard bowls, a skillet,
  and in two cases a small pot for eggs.
- Every recipe stays under 50 minutes of active preparation and cooking.
- Caesar is the only raw-emulsion style, but it explicitly requires a
  pasteurized yolk. No salad is a special-occasion project.
- Cobb and chef salads use hard-boiled eggs; ranch and Buffalo salads use one
  skillet for chicken; none hides deep-frying, multiple breading stations, or
  elaborate garnishes.
- All five are desirable household or restaurant favorites and none is retained
  merely to fill American salad coverage.

## Safety and execution checks

- Caesar requires pasteurized yolk, completely dry romaine, cooled croutons,
  and immediate service.
- Cobb and chef eggs are fully set with an 11-minute covered stand and ice-bath
  cooling.
- Cobb, ranch, and Buffalo chicken reaches 165°F and rests before cutting.
- Warm chicken remains separate from cold greens until plating. Dressings and
  crunchy toppings stay separate for meal prep.
- Tomatoes are halved or seeded to control water; all lettuces are thoroughly
  dried before assembly.
- Every listed ingredient is allocated to preparation, cooking, finishing, or
  service.

## Highest-rated and authority pattern synthesis

- Caesar combines 4.1/5 across 1,559 ratings and 5/5 across 153 votes with a
  four-recipe comparison whose historically informed winner scored 10/10.
  Crisp romaine, a light anchovy-yolk-lemon emulsion, Parmesan, garlic croutons,
  and last-minute tossing recur.
- Cobb follows 4.93/5 across 26 reviews plus two editorial classic references.
  Chicken, bacon, avocado, tomato, egg, blue cheese, mixed greens, tart
  vinaigrette, and composed presentation define the result.
- Chef salad combines 5/5 across 16 votes and 5/5 across two ratings with a Food
  Network test-kitchen pattern. Cold lettuce, ham, turkey, two cheeses, egg,
  tomato, cucumber, and creamy dressing recur.
- Chicken ranch uses a 4.84/5 chopped-salad pattern and a 130-rating meal-kit
  dataset. Whole-cut chicken, crisp vegetables, fresh ranch, and separately
  stored components work; extra bacon and cooked breadcrumbs were removed after
  reviewers identified needless steps.
- Buffalo chicken follows 4.8/5 across 22 ratings plus two poultry and editorial
  sources. Hot-sauce chicken, crisp romaine, celery, carrots, and cool
  blue-cheese dressing recur.
