## Identity checks

- Meen moilee is a mild, pale Kerala coconut fish stew with green chile, ginger, curry leaves, and low-heat finishing, distinct from the preceding red, tamarind-led Goan curry.
- Chettinad chicken depends on a freshly roasted and ground spice-coconut masala, with fennel, pepper, curry leaf, and optional kalpasi providing regional specificity.
- Hyderabadi chicken biryani uses a kacchi construction: raw overnight-marinated chicken sits below partially cooked rice and finishes under sealed dum.
- Lucknowi lamb biryani uses the contrasting Awadhi pakki construction: tender lamb yakhni and rice are cooked separately before a restrained, perfumed dum.
- Chingri malai curry keeps large Bengali prawns central in a subtle coconut sauce and treats the likely Malay-curry name lineage transparently.

## Safety and execution checks

- Meen moilee and chingri malai curry both use 145°F seafood endpoints, minimal agitation, and low heat for thick coconut milk.
- Chettinad chicken reaches 165°F at the bone, and its whole spices and coconut are roasted only to fragrant/deep gold rather than burnt.
- Hyderabadi kacchi biryani requires a 165°F thermometer check in the bottom chicken layer after sealed dum; rice is intentionally only 70 percent cooked before layering.
- Lucknowi lamb is fork-tender and beyond 145°F before layering, because the short final dum is for integration rather than tenderization.
- Both biryanis specify opening the steam-filled pot away from the face.
