## Identity checks

- Bean and cheese quesadillas use fresh folded corn masa, thick pinto beans, Oaxaca cheese, and roasted tomatillo salsa. They are not two flour tortillas presented as a sincronizada.
- Baja fish tacos follow the Ensenada pattern: light battered white fish, corn tortilla, fine dry cabbage, salsa mexicana, crema, and lime assembled at the table.
- Cheese and bean pupusas enclose cold firm red beans and melting cheese in thick masa and retain the Salvadoran companions of curtido and cooked tomato salsa.
- Bean and egg baleadas use large scratch flour tortillas, fried red beans, soft eggs, salty cheese, crema, and avocado. The note distinguishes the mixta from the simpler base form.
- Reina pepiada arepas use precooked white cornmeal and a cool chicken-avocado-mayonnaise filling with a small amount of peas tied to accounts of the original.

## Safety and execution checks

- Beans for all three bean-led recipes are cooked before the recipe begins and reduced to a stable filling texture.
- Fish fries in uncrowded 375°F oil and reaches 145°F; toppings remain separate until service.
- Pupusa beans cool before mixing with cheese, the masa stays covered, and the filled cakes cook through with repeated turns.
- Baleada eggs remain soft but fully set, and finished tortillas are stacked under a towel rather than held uncovered.
- Chicken breast reaches 165°F before cooling and shredding; avocado is folded in only after the chicken is cool.

## Highest-rated pattern synthesis

- Explicit conventional rating data is sparse for this regional group. Mexico in My Kitchen’s corn-dough quesadillas at 4.5 from 2 votes support fresh soft masa, Oaxaca cheese, and moderate comal heat, but the small pool is not treated as conclusive.
- Mexican Food Journal’s foundational frijoles de la olla at 3.88 from 16 votes supports a broad Mexican bean method; its value here is technique and cultural grounding more than a high score.
- Ensenada fish-taco decisions rely most heavily on Rick Bayless’s interviews with working Ensenada cooks, which explain why water or beer batter, 375°F oil, simple cabbage, salsa, and crema repeatedly succeed.
- Salvadoran, Honduran, and Venezuelan recipes prioritize cooks from those communities and regional publishers because general-purpose high-star listings often flatten the wrappers and condiments that define the dishes.

## Carry-forward rules

- Scratch the defining masa or flour wrapper when its texture determines the dish.
- A low-volume rating is a supporting signal, not proof of consensus.
- For filled griddle breads, filling temperature and viscosity must be audited before shaping.
- Preserve culturally distinct table companions instead of substituting a universal slaw or salsa.
