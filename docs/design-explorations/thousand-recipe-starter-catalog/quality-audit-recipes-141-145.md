## Identity checks

- Beef arayes spread Lebanese-spiced kafta thinly inside sturdy pita and use a grill-pan-to-oven finish for a crisp shell and safely cooked center.
- Chicken gyro uses tightly stacked and roasted thighs that are shaved and briefly crisped, not generic grilled chicken strips.
- Pork souvlaki remains distinct cubes on skewers with a short oregano-lemon marinade, followed by a traditional onion-and-lemon rest.
- Caprese panini keeps tomato, fresh mozzarella, and basil primary, using only a restrained scratch pesto and no heavy glaze.
- Mortadella focaccia authors the bread and uses protected Mortadella Bologna IGP, burrata, and savory pistachio cream in loose layers.

## Safety and execution checks

- Arayes ground beef reaches 160°F after initial controlled browning.
- Chicken gyro reaches 165°F while still in its stack; shaving and broiling happen only afterward.
- Pork souvlaki reaches 145°F and rests 5 minutes.
- Tomato, mozzarella, cucumber, and burrata are explicitly drained or dried to prevent soggy bread and sauces.
- Focaccia cools on a rack before slicing; hot bread is not compressed around chilled dairy and salume.

## Highest-rated pattern synthesis

- Arayes draws on a Lebanese family recipe rated 5/5 by 267 cooks; its distinctive improvement is controlling vegetable moisture and spreading the meat evenly.
- Chicken gyro draws on a Greek recipe rated 4.82/5 across 489 votes; sustained marination, tight stacking, full cooking, thin carving, and a cold thick tzatziki are its repeated advantages.
- Pork souvlaki draws on 970 votes at 4.60/5. The pattern is simple and durable: pork neck or shoulder, short acid exposure, hot grilling, lemon-onion rest, and warm pita.
- Caprese sources have much smaller rating pools; convergence on ripe tomato, fresh mozzarella, basil, moderate pressing heat, and moisture control matters more than the nominal 5/5 result.
- Mortadella evidence comes primarily from the Italian protection consortium and Italian editorial kitchens, where provenance and ingredient handling are more probative than consumer star counts.

## Carry-forward rules

- Do not collapse stacked shaved meat and discrete skewered meat into the same home technique.
- In enclosed raw-meat breads, filling thickness and a measured endpoint are inseparable safety instructions.
- Moisture management is a defining sandwich technique, not incidental prep.
- For protected regional products, source provenance can be more important than adding scratch complexity elsewhere.
