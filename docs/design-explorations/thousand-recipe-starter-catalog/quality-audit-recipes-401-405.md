## Familiar naming and meal stories

- Korean spicy chicken and cabbage skillet leads before `dakgalbi`; its story
  explains the communal Chuncheon meal and why this focused version omits rice
  cakes that belong to the separate tteokbokki dinner.
- Korean glass noodles with beef and vegetables leads before `japchae`; its
  story explains the dish's vegetable-first name and the efficient grouped-cook
  adaptation.
- Korean soy-braised beef short ribs leads before `galbi jjim`; its story
  identifies the celebration role and distinguishes defining cleanup and
  braising from optional ceremonial garnishes.
- Korean tender pork lettuce wraps leads before `bossam`; its story explains
  kimjang context and discloses prepared kimchi and ssamjang as the practical
  alternative to hiding multiple condiment recipes.
- Korean spicy rice cakes with fish cakes and eggs leads before `tteokbokki`;
  its story keeps the recognizable street-food core while explaining the modest
  additions that make it dinner.

## Familiarity and effort check

- Dakgalbi is a roughly 50-minute one-skillet chicken-and-vegetable dinner.
- Japchae uses one noodle pot and one skillet; compatible vegetables cook
  together while beef and noodles retain separate texture protection.
- Bossam is mostly passive simmering with prepared fermented condiments and an
  interactive lettuce-wrap service.
- Tteokbokki is a 35-minute one-pan dinner using Korean-market rice cakes and
  fish cakes, plus familiar eggs and cabbage.
- Galbi jjim is the sole special-occasion project: soaking, blanching, fat
  control, a long braise, delayed vegetables, and final reduction. Its burden is
  explicit rather than disguised as easy.
- The batch has no discovery recipes and no dish retained merely for Korean
  cuisine coverage.

## Safety and execution checks

- Dakgalbi chicken reaches 165°F; thin sweet potato cooks beneath covered steam
  before the sauce is reduced uncovered.
- Japchae beef reaches at least 145°F. Noodles are drained and sauced promptly,
  and spinach cooks fully during the final toss.
- Galbi jjim ribs are soaked, blanched, rinsed, skimmed, and braised until a
  skewer enters easily. Surface fat is removed before final reduction.
- Bossam pork shoulder reaches at least 190°F for sliceable tenderness, rests in
  its cooking liquid, and is sliced only before service.
- Tteokbokki uses prepared stock, fully cooked fish cakes, and hard-boiled eggs.
  Sauce consistency is judged only after rice cakes release starch.
- Every listed ingredient is allocated to preparation, cooking, finishing, or
  service.
