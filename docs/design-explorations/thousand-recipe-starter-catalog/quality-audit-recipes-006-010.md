# Quality Audit: Recipes 006–010

Recorded: 2026-08-06

Recipes reviewed: Chicken and waffles, Shrimp and grits, Corned beef hash with
eggs, Denver omelet, and Breakfast burritos.

## Audit dimensions

- Is this a genuinely independent recipe rather than a title variation?
- Does each ingredient appear in the method, in a coherent quantity?
- Do time, yield, heat, and doneness cues agree?
- Do the research findings explain success and failure rather than merely list
  sources?
- Is the cultural or regional label narrower than the evidence supports?
- Would a normal home cook know how to recover texture at the likely failure
  points?
- Is the proof state honest?

## Findings and corrections

### Chicken and waffles

Strong: the recipe protects the two textures separately, uses a hydrated
cornstarch dredge, names a safe thigh endpoint, and controls the final sweetness.

Correction made: waffle salt appeared in the method but was not separately
allocated in the ingredient list. It now has its own measured line.

Remaining proof gap: frying time and the cornmeal-waffle ratio need a real cook.

### Shrimp and grits

Strong: the recipe treats stone-ground grits by texture rather than package
time, removes shrimp before the sauce, and uses acid instead of a heavy cream
gravy. The Lowcountry label is supported without claiming one canonical version.

Remaining proof gap: the water requirement will vary materially by grit mill.
The method tells the cook how to adapt, but a cooked-once note must record the
tested brand and actual liquid.

### Corned beef hash with eggs

Strong: steam-dried potatoes, large-section turning, and late salt directly
answer the common soggy-hash and over-salting failures. Eggs make the stated
four-serving breakfast complete.

Remaining proof gap: the 12-inch skillet capacity and five-to-seven-minute crust
window need a real cook on the target stove.

### Denver omelet

Strong: the recipe distinguishes diner-style browning from French-omelet
texture, cooks watery vegetables first, and avoids an overfilled six-egg fold.

Correction made: the first draft's butter wording did not reserve fat for the
second omelet. The ingredient quantity and per-stage allocation are now exact.

Remaining proof gap: an eight-inch pan may feel crowded for three eggs plus
filling; verify against the actual Kwilt test kitchen pan and move to ten inches
if folding suffers.

### Breakfast burritos

Strong: the recipe explicitly manages wet salsa, carryover eggs, tortilla
warming, cooling for freezing, and fresh-versus-freezer avocado behavior. It is
not padded with rice.

Remaining proof gap: tortilla brand and nominal diameter affect safe filling
capacity; record weight-per-burrito during the first cook.

## Verdict

The five recipes are coming through materially better than the original
catalog: each has a dish-specific technique, failure model, adaptation decision,
and ingredient/method structure. The audit also caught two errors that schema
validation alone could not. That means the workflow is viable, but automated
shape checks are not enough; the manual ingredient-accounting and cookware
audit must remain mandatory for every batch.

All five remain `desk-reviewed`. None should be represented as kitchen-tested
or moved into the live catalog yet.
