## Identity checks

- Arepa con queso uses Colombian-style precooked white cornmeal with salty cheese in the dough and mozzarella enclosed. It is thinner and cheese-led rather than a split, heavily filled Venezuelan arepa.
- Choripán uses mild fresh Argentine chorizo, crisp bread, and knife-chopped rested chimichurri without cilantro, tomato, or a blender.
- Lomito completo uses a thin beef cutlet, ham, melted cheese, fried egg, lettuce, tomato, and mayonnaise in the recognizable Argentine fast-food structure.
- Peruvian butifarra authors jamón del país with achiote, ají panca, garlic, cumin, oregano, and vinegar, then pairs it with drained salsa criolla and lettuce.
- The Bauru follows the documented Ponto Chic original: roast beef, a molten cheese blend, tomato, and pickle in hollowed pão francês—not ham and cheese.

## Safety and execution checks

- Thick cheese arepas cook over medium-low until their sidewalls firm; a brief covered finish is used before any increase in heat.
- Fresh chorizo cooks gently before direct browning and reaches 160°F.
- Lomito beef reaches 145°F, eggs have fully set whites, and washed lettuce is completely dry.
- Pork loin for jamón del país reaches 145°F, rests, and cools before slicing; salsa criolla is drained before use.
- Bauru roast beef reaches 145°F and rests before chilling for safe, paper-thin slicing; cheese melts over water instead of direct heat.

## Highest-rated pattern synthesis

- These regional sandwich sources generally do not publish conventional star-and-count pairs, so the audit does not manufacture a numerical hierarchy.
- For arepa con queso, the strongest success signal is convergence across Colombian sources and a Colombian cooking demonstration viewed millions of times: precooked white cornmeal, salty cheese in the dough, melting cheese inside, and patient griddle heat.
- Argentine sources converge on a minimal choripán, while their repeated warnings explain quality: mild fresh sausage, staged grilling, crusty bread, and rough rested chimichurri.
- Peruvian sources agree that jamón del país—not ordinary deli ham—is the quality center of a butifarra and that salsa criolla supplies freshness.
- Brazilian regional media, broadcaster recipes, and the protected formula all contradict the common ham version of Bauru and support roast beef, cheese, tomato, pickle, and hollowed bread.

## Carry-forward rules

- When a dish has a legally or institutionally documented original, prefer that evidence over widespread renamed variants.
- Do not add seasoning simply to exhaust an ingredient quantity; remove the unneeded quantity.
- For slice-led sandwiches, cooling and cross-grain slicing are first-class technique.
- Keep related arepas distinct through thickness, dough composition, cheese placement, and service style.
