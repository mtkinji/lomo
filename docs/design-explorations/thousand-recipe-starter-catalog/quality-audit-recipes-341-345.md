- Duck legs cure under refrigeration, cook until collagen-tender, and reheat through to 165°F before serving.
- Steak reaches 145°F with a three-minute rest, potatoes have explicit frying temperatures, and cognac is added with the pan off the flame.
- Chicken thighs reach 175°F for tenderness; brandy is likewise added off the flame.
- Veal reaches at least 145°F and remains at a gentle simmer; pasteurized yolks are used and the liaison reaches 160°F without boiling.
- Every listed ingredient is allocated to preparation, cooking, or service.

- Steak frites with peppercorn sauce is a highly recognizable household anchor. Its 75-minute timeline and double-fry method keep it off quick-weeknight shelves, but the meal itself is not niche.
- Chicken chasseur is an 80-minute cuisine anchor whose underlying form—braised chicken with mushrooms and pan sauce—is familiar and achievable in one skillet.
- Blanquette de veau is a 2-hour-40-minute cuisine anchor. It is a traditional French staple and a recognizable creamy stew form, but it must be presented as a weekend meal rather than an everyday default.
- Duck confit is the sole discovery dish in this group: it requires a 12-hour cure, several hours of cooking, and substantial duck fat. It belongs only in weekend, project-cooking, or special-occasion discovery surfaces.
