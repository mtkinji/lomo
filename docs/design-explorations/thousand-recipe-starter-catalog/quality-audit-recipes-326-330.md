## Identity checks

- Osaka-style okonomiyaki uses the region's mixed, cabbage-forward method with dashi, nagaimo, tenkasu, pork belly, sauce, mayonnaise, aonori, and bonito; it is not flattened into Hiroshima's layered noodle version.
- Sukiyaki uses Kanto warishita in a shallow communal pot and distinguishes the customary pasteurized-egg dip from the cooking liquid.
- Shabu-shabu remains distinct from sukiyaki through clear unsalted kombu broth, paper-thin meat, individual ponzu and sesame dips, and an udon finish.
- Nikujaga preserves its ordinary Japanese meat-and-potatoes identity, drop-lid simmer, intact vegetables, and flavor-deepening rest.
- Salt-grilled mackerel remains deliberately spare: mackerel, sake, salt, crisp skin, grated daikon, soy, and lemon.

## Safety and execution checks

- Okonomiyaki pork reaches 145°F and rests three minutes; modest 6-inch pancakes reduce broken-flip risk.
- Both tabletop hot pots require a stable ventilated burner, separate raw and cooked utensils, small-batch cooking, and beef at 145°F. Sukiyaki uses pasteurized egg only.
- Nikujaga beef reaches 145°F before its staged removal; returning it late prevents a long simmer from toughening thin slices.
- Mackerel reaches 145°F under continuous broiler observation; blotting and a raised rack reduce steaming and flare risk.
- Every listed ingredient is allocated to a preparation, cooking, or serving instruction.

## Familiarity and effort check

- Three of five recipes are cuisine anchors: okonomiyaki uses one skillet, nikujaga is an approachable one-pot meat-and-potatoes dinner, and salt-grilled mackerel has only seven ingredients.
- Sukiyaki and shabu-shabu are discovery-tier occasion meals. Their communal setup, specialty slicing, tabletop burner, and raw/cooked utensil management make them inappropriate for default weeknight shelves.
- No recipe is mislabeled as quick: active-plus-inactive times range from 35 minutes for mackerel to 90 minutes for shabu-shabu.
- This batch adds two complex/social meals but three simpler counterweights. The staging tiers and eventual shelves will keep the occasion meals available without making them the face of the catalog.

## Highest-rated pattern synthesis

- Okonomiyaki's 4.74/5 across 724 votes is paired with Japan's official Osaka method; both emphasize minimal just-in-time mixing as the decisive texture control.
- Sukiyaki's 4.75/5 across 285 votes supports the warishita, marbled beef, tofu, vegetables, shirataki, rice, and pasteurized-egg option; Japanese historical and home sources confirm the social identity and regional variation.
- Shabu-shabu's 4.71/5 across 98 votes supports clear kombu broth, thin meat, two dips, vegetables, tofu, and noodles; two Japanese sources reinforce its contrast with sweet-soy sukiyaki.
- Nikujaga's 4.72/5 across 195 votes supports the drop lid and rest, while two Japanese sources establish it as an everyday home dish rather than restaurant spectacle.
- Salt-grilled mackerel's 4.74/5 across 149 votes validates the minimal sake-salt pattern; Japanese home and culinary sources corroborate salt resting, blotting, grilling, and grated daikon.
