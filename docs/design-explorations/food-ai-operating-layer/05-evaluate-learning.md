# Evaluate Learning: AI-Native Food

## Keep and deepen when

- a real recipe normally reaches clean save faster than manual entry;
- the user can correct uncertain fields without hunting through the source;
- saved Recipes are cooked from and reused in later planning cycles;
- AI plan proposals reduce blank-page work and users can explain why a
  suggestion appeared;
- grocery compilation requires materially fewer corrections than manual list
  reconstruction;
- proposals and receipts make AI operations easier to trust, not more tedious;
  and
- users ask AI to perform more registered food operations after the first
  successful cycle.

## Revise when

- high-confidence presentation causes users to miss transcription mistakes;
- review requires reading every field regardless of confidence;
- planning suggestions feel generic because the evidence boundary is too thin;
- Chat and native Food surfaces disagree about state or permissions;
- the confirmation model creates repetitive ceremony for reversible work; or
- public attribution choices expose private identity or confuse source credit
  with authorship.

## Stop or narrow when

- users prefer a photo attachment to structured Recipes after repeated tries;
- import correction approaches manual transcription burden;
- the integrated plan-to-list transition does not improve repeated household
  cycles; or
- reliable execution would require unsupported retailer, rights, or financial
  authority.

## Evidence review

For every dogfood cycle, inspect the full path: source artifact, extracted
draft, corrections, immutable Recipe, planning proposal and rationale,
finalized plan version, compiled GroceryList provenance, external handoff
receipt, and user-reported outcome. Do not infer success from model evaluation,
source tests, or a generated retailer link alone.
