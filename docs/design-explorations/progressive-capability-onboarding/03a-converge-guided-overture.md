# Re-Converge: The Guided Overture

**Status:** Selected direction for learning-release planning. This supersedes the pure
Agent-hosted task-router decision as the leading first-run concept; it does not authorize replacing
the current onboarding yet.

## Decision

Choose the **Guided Overture** as the leading expression of Guided Relevance:

```text
SHOW a short sequence of concrete task transformations
  -> SETTLE those same scenes into stable task choices
    -> CHOOSE one, name something else, or look around
      -> let the owning capability produce first value
```

Use **Stage, Then Settle** as the primary motion hypothesis. Use the stable choice surface as the
reduced-motion and accessibility-complete experience.

Build the overture as a scalable stage system rather than a fixed film:

- capabilities own truthful orientation offers, native entry, boundaries, and first value;
- the shell owns selection, presentation budget, choreography, and reorientation;
- the short overture remains approximately five or six beats as the portfolio grows; and
- Agent hosts ambiguity and **Something else**, but no model response is required to orient or
  choose.

## Why This Wins

The pure Agent-hosted task router solved the blank-prompt problem but did not communicate the
breadth of a life-assistance suite. A capability grid communicates breadth but asks users to
understand Kwilt's internal product nouns. A traditional onboarding carousel explains features
slowly and then throws away the explanation surface.

The Guided Overture combines orientation and action:

- quick task transformations make different forms of help recognizable;
- repeated visual grammar makes the portfolio feel like one Kwilt;
- the demonstrated scenes become the actual choices;
- concrete task language avoids broad lifestyle promises; and
- simple native choreography can create energy without video or bespoke animation.

## Product Boundary

The selected concept does not mean every capability appears in first-run.

> **Adding capabilities expands the candidate library, not the length of onboarding.**

A capability can be first-class in the full map and Agent routing without occupying one of the
overture's limited beats. Eligibility for the opening is an editorial and product-truth decision,
not automatic promotion.

## Capability Delta

Today, an unscoped user can encounter Goals-shaped onboarding, a task chooser, a capability menu,
or Agent, but none of those alone demonstrates the suite's breadth and turns that demonstration
into a guided first action.

After this concept ships, an unscoped user can:

- recognize several materially different forms of Kwilt help in seconds;
- interrupt the sequence as soon as one task feels relevant;
- choose from the exact task transformations they just saw;
- state a different need without facing a blank Agent first; and
- enter a native first-value path without learning Kwilt's product taxonomy.

Still intentionally unsupported:

- showing every capability in first-run;
- promising unavailable capabilities;
- choosing or configuring the whole suite;
- using sensitive inference to decide what someone should need; and
- replaying mandatory onboarding whenever the portfolio changes.

## Reductive Decisions

- One shared stage and bounded scene language, not custom animation per capability.
- One primary orientation offer per capability in the initial contract.
- At most one offer per capability in the short overture.
- One stable choice surface shared by animated, reduced-motion, and screen-reader experiences.
- One deterministic composition for the first learning release, not an AI ranking engine.
- No new global permissions, household setup, demographic survey, or completion checklist.
- No production onboarding replacement until the concept has passed an isolated learning release.

## Accepted Trade-Offs

- The first composition will be editorial rather than fully adaptive.
- Five or six beats cannot explain the complete suite.
- A concept study may be needed before all portfolio capabilities are active in one build.
- The most memorable motion may need to be simplified if comprehension or accessibility suffers.

## Rejected Trade-Offs

- Lengthening the sequence whenever a capability is added.
- Showing unbuilt capabilities as tappable promises to real users.
- Requiring users to finish the montage before choosing.
- Replacing the current onboarding globally to obtain early learning.
- Treating a participant's positive reaction to a concept montage as proof that native activation
  works.

## Stated Bet

We're betting that **Show -> Settle -> Choose** gives unscoped users the missing bearings of a
broad suite while remaining fast, task-focused, and actionable.

We're also betting that most of the concept risk can be tested in a parallel, internal experience
before Kwilt changes the automatic first-run path.

If the montage is memorable but people cannot say what Kwilt does or predict what a button will do,
we should simplify the scene grammar and composition. We should not compensate with more slides or
marketing explanation.
