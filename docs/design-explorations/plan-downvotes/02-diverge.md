# Diverge: Plan downvotes

Axis of variation: who can see disagreement and how strongly it affects the
shared shortlist.

## A. Signed downvote

Add a down-arrow beside the up-arrow. Tapping it records one attributable
negative response. Everyone can see the count and reveal who downvoted. A
person may have either one positive reaction or one downvote, never both.

- Best when: the household is comfortable with direct, shared opinions.
- Fails when: a visible negative response feels like rejection of the nominator.
- System fit: smallest extension of the existing reaction row and one-reaction constraint.
- Anti-pattern check: passes only if it stays advisory and does not auto-remove.

## B. Private “not for me”

Show the same down-arrow, but only the responder and organizers see negative
responses. Other members continue to see positive support. The organizer sees a
quiet negative count and names when deciding what to shop for.

- Best when: household members need psychological safety or include children.
- Fails when: hidden asymmetry makes the shared board feel inconsistent.
- System fit: requires role-shaped projections and more explanation.
- Anti-pattern check: strongest privacy fit, but risks invisible rules.

## C. Organizer-visible hard veto

Treat the down-arrow as “cannot eat / please do not choose,” optionally asking
for a reason such as allergy or strong aversion.

- Best when: the signal represents a genuine constraint.
- Fails when: ordinary preference is mistaken for safety or authority.
- System fit: requires a new constraint model, reason handling, and decision rules.
- Anti-pattern check: fails the bounded request by turning a reaction into workflow.

## D. Net Reddit score

Expose one combined score where positive reactions add one and downvotes
subtract one. Sort Ideas by that signed score.

- Best when: users already understand public voting mechanics.
- Fails when: the score erases whether a recipe is loved by five people and
  rejected by four, which is crucial household context.
- System fit: simple computation but a lossy presentation.
- Anti-pattern check: risks gamifying dinner and silently turning participation into majority rule.
