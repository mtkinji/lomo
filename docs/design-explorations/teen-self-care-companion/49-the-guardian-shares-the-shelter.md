# Pet Engine Study 45 — The Guardian Shares the Shelter

## Frame and system alignment

> The tiny world should feel like a beautifully directed anime that I can play
> inside, and Moss should grow from a very different baby into a powerful adult.

When a storm enters the meadow, Charlie wants maturity to change what Moss is
capable of doing—not merely its sprite size—so that growing up feels like
becoming more present and protective inside a living world.

The target remains the provisional teen participant Charlie within
`audience-aspirational-family-organizers`. The hero demand is ordinary life
moving forward without turning Kwilt into a productivity hobby. This study most
directly serves the underserved “notice non-metric progress” step from
`job-flow-sarah-see-who-im-becoming`, currently scored 3: the Guardian's growth
should be understood through behavior rather than a level, meter, or claim.

Constraint posture: **Extend the existing portable world engine.** Weather,
touch, camera, wildlife, Focus priority, stage manifests, and the old tree stay
authoritative. Add one bounded rain relationship without a rescue score,
morality prompt, collectible, need meter, or new art pipeline.

**Design challenge:** How might Guardian Moss turn a rainstorm into one
player-authored act of care, while preserving calm weather causality and the
freedom to simply watch?

## Yes-and check

Broader expansion is intentionally skipped. This could eventually become a
vocabulary of mature, relationship-shaped world behaviors, but the next useful
proof is one complete sentence: a Guardian notices smaller life in the storm,
the person chooses to help, and both reach shelter together.

## Divergence

### Let Guardian stand against the storm

Guardian could remain in the open while Baby and Young seek shelter. This makes
adult strength legible, but the scene is mostly a pose and can confuse power
with ignoring physical needs.

### Let the person choose shelter or rain

Rain could pause at a decision point where the person taps the tree or meadow.
This adds direct authorship, but it turns weather into a repeated binary prompt
and gives every storm interface weight.

### Let Guardian share the shelter

Only Guardian notices a rain-heavy firefly struggling close to the ground. The
camera holds both. The person may tap the firefly; Guardian approaches, lowers
its body, lets the visitor settle into its leafy mantle, then carries it beneath
the old tree. If the person does nothing, Guardian quietly makes the same caring
choice after a generous pause. Baby and Young keep their existing self-shelter
story.

## Convergence

Choose **let Guardian share the shelter**.

The authored sentence is:

1. rain arrives through the existing weather transition;
2. Baby and Young flinch and seek the old tree as they do today;
3. Guardian notices one wet firefly flying low, and the camera composes both;
4. the world waits up to six quiet seconds for the person's tap;
5. Guardian plants, approaches the exact visitor, and lowers for contact;
6. the visitor settles behind Guardian's shoulder rather than teleporting;
7. Guardian carries it on the terrain to the existing shelter anchor;
8. both hold beneath the tree while the rain remains;
9. new weather, Focus, reset, or Reduce Motion resolves the episode safely.

We're betting that **adult power expressed as care** will make evolution feel
more emotionally meaningful and anime-like than another larger jump. If it
reads as a mission marker, forced moral lesson, or decorative follower, revisit
the invitation and contact performance before generalizing the vocabulary.

## Capability delta and reductive decisions

Today, every stage ultimately shelters itself from rain. After this study,
Guardian can recognize vulnerable habitat life and the person can author one
complete shared-shelter scene. The Pet still cannot control weather, earn rescue
credit, collect wildlife, receive praise, lose care, or open a quest log.

The interaction reuses the existing rain control, old-tree anchor, firefly
drawing vocabulary, walking locomotion, camera director, and capability canvas.
No new button is added. The firefly itself is the invitation; keyboard access
uses Enter while the invitation is active.

## Learning release and evaluation

The private mobile-first site remains the learning channel. At 390 × 844,
trigger Rain as Baby, Young, and Guardian. Verify only Guardian receives the
visitor scene; the wet firefly stays visible and low; a direct tap starts the
approach; the visitor does not jump onto Moss before body contact; Moss faces
and walks forward throughout; the firefly remains attached during travel; both
are grounded and legible under the old tree; and ordinary touch returns when
weather changes. Repeat without tapping to verify the calm autonomous choice.

Verify Focus clears the invitation and owns the old tree. Under Reduce Motion,
resolve directly to one static Guardian-and-firefly shelter tableau. No private
data or new analytics are required; the learning is Andrew's direct play and
whether the scene feels authored rather than procedural.

Disconfirming evidence is backward walking, a floating or detached passenger,
the firefly hidden behind the dock, a prompt card, a rescue fanfare, a visitor
appearing for Baby or Young, abandonment in an intermediate state, or Guardian
looking less physically affected by rain than the smaller forms.

## Spec refinement

Pure world state owns a transient `rainGuest` with quiet, waiting, carried, and
sheltered phases; hit testing; player and timeout activation; contact timing;
attachment; shelter arrival; weather/Focus cleanup; camera framing; and Reduce
Motion meaning. Tests precede implementation. Canvas draws the existing wet
firefly vocabulary at the portable guest coordinate and arbitrates its touch
before ordinary meadow intent. No user-owned product decision remains for this
prototype study.
