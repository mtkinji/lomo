# Evaluate Learning: Pixel Pet Labs

## Evidence to collect

Ask participants to show, not just tell:

- Which pet did you choose, and why?
- Did naming and palette choice make it feel like yours?
- When did you visit Pet without being prompted?
- Did feeding feel like a pleasant receipt or like another obligation?
- Did Pet ever make you create trivial tasks or complete something only for the reward?
- After missing a day, did returning feel neutral, warm, or guilty?
- Could you tell what the Pet was expressing without words?
- Which sounds made the Pet feel alive, and which became annoying?
- Did the first evolution feel meaningful enough to keep?
- Did Chat help when Pet surfaced there, or did it feel like an ad for another capability?

During the site prototype, also observe:

- whether participants understand the care loop without explanation;
- which parts they repeatedly replay using prototype controls;
- whether they return through the link later without being asked;
- whether accelerated evolution produces delight or merely completes a test;
- whether the experience still feels appealing after the debug controls are closed;
- whether participants ask to connect a real Kwilt action, which is stronger evidence than asking them if integration sounds useful.

## Success signals

- People voluntarily revisit Pet after setup rather than only entering through a prompt.
- At least some participants can name their pet's character and describe why they chose it.
- A care moment makes a real completion feel warmer without changing the meaning of the work.
- Participants understand that missing a day cannot hurt the Pet.
- The five types produce real preference rather than one obvious favorite and four fillers.
- Reaching the first evolution increases affection or curiosity.
- Sounds are retained by people who enjoy them and can be disabled without losing meaning.
- Chat handoffs feel contextual and do not make Pet feel scattered across the timeline.

## Disconfirming evidence

- Most people enable Pet, choose one, and rarely return.
- People need meters, reminders, or guilt to understand what to do.
- Users manufacture trivial completions to feed the Pet.
- The Pet reads as childish to teens or emotionally manipulative to parents.
- Five types still feel interchangeable or one type dominates overwhelmingly.
- The nonverbal Pet is too opaque, or Chat explanations make it feel like a chatbot in disguise.
- The first evolution feels merely cosmetic rather than like a record of showing up.
- Sound is muted immediately because it is repetitive or socially awkward.

## Decisions after the test

### Keep iterating on the site

Stay in the site prototype while the main uncertainty concerns pet appeal, animation, sound, care interaction, habitat, naming, or evolution. These are cheaper and more truthful to refine before app integration.

### Graduate to Kwilt Labs

Move toward app implementation only when participants voluntarily return, care about their chosen Pet, understand the no-harm care loop, and explicitly want real To-do or Focus activity to affect it. At that point, write the app-integration plan against the validated prototype rather than treating prototype code as production architecture.

### Continue the core

Continue if choice, daily care, and first evolution create voluntary attachment without pressure. Improve reactions and care receipts before adding breadth.

### Deepen personalization

Add one small customization layer only if users clearly ask to make the Pet more theirs. Prefer palettes, a chosen habitat object, or a user-selected evolution branch before a store or large accessory catalog.

### Expand capability connections

Connect another capability only if participants can explain how the existing To-do and Focus connections helped them. Explore discoveries or Chapter milestone portraits are the strongest next candidates. Money and Screen Time remain excluded.

### Retire or reposition

If people enjoy the art but the Pet does not affect healthy follow-through, reposition it as optional delight rather than an engagement system. If it creates guilt or fake work, retire the care mechanic rather than tuning the pressure upward.

## Reflection update

The site prototype alone does not change Kwilt's job-flow delivery score. If the experiment later ships beyond Labs, update the relevant job-flow delivery evidence for family participation and “keep using the system because it feels helpful, not fussy.” Add a canonical teen audience/persona and job flow only when real teen evidence is strong enough to justify it.
