# Life Progress Blooms

## Why this is the next study

The Pet is meant to be an engagement device for Kwilt's real capabilities, not a parallel self-care game. Study 14 makes Moss act convincingly inside weather. Focus already creates a shared quiet ritual, and Play together creates breeze plus wildlife. Completing a To-do, however, still changes mostly copy and unlocks care. The habitat does not visibly understand that something in life moved forward.

Study 15 tests one calm rule: **when a real intention becomes action, the world remembers beautifully.**

## Design challenge

How might completing an ordinary Kwilt To-do cause one beautiful, inspectable event in Moss's world—without coins, XP, streak pressure, collectibles, or the implication that Moss was harmed before the task was done?

## Convergence: one progress bloom

The first completed To-do of a prototype day opens one small flower patch in the habitat:

1. A bud emerges at a bounded nearby world coordinate.
2. Moss notices the habitat event.
3. Moss pads toward it using ordinary authored locomotion.
4. Moss lowers into an approved nuzzle/admiration performance.
5. The bloom remains as quiet scenery after the performance ends.

The world owns the bloom position, opening progress, source class, and Pet response. The renderer owns only the platform-specific pixels. Direct touch can interrupt Moss, Focus can take priority, and weather can continue around the patch. The bloom itself remains; it is memory, not a task target that expires.

## Capability grammar

- **To-do:** opens a progress bloom and invites Moss to inspect it.
- **Focus:** settles Moss beneath the old tree for quiet company.
- **Play together:** stirs breeze and invites stage-appropriate wildlife.
- **Care:** completes the day's one bounded care moment and advances long-term growth.

These are different world consequences for different real-life meanings. They all use the same capability-owned, source-class receipt boundary and never expose task titles, notes, performance history, or household surveillance.

## Reductive decisions

- One bounded flower patch per To-do command; keep at most three visible patches in the prototype world.
- No flower count, collection, inventory, garden screen, rarity, naming, harvesting, or exchange.
- No bonus for opening the app and no loss when a day is quiet.
- No task title or private content enters the Pet engine.
- No new character art; use the approved discover, walk, and care vocabulary.
- No forced camera cut. Ordinary destination-side follow frames the event.

## Acceptance evidence

- Pure world tests prove deterministic placement, bounded patch count, opening progress, notice → travel → admire sequence, interruption, and Focus priority.
- Renderer tests prove the patch lives in world coordinates and is not UI furniture.
- Phone-scale browser review proves the opening reads inside the meadow and Moss remains grounded while approaching it.
- Completing the To-do still produces only the day's existing care opportunity; the bloom adds no parallel score or reward.
- Focus, Play, weather, wildlife, evolution, Reduce Motion, and direct touch remain operational.

## Bet

We are betting that **a persistent world memory is more emotionally connective than another celebration animation**. If the bloom reads as payment for a task, the next revision should make it subtler or remove persistence—not add value, rarity, or more rewards.
