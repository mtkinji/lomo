# Diverge: Stitch Five

## Fixed frame

All alternatives preserve the familiar five-dice, three-roll, thirteen-category rules. The axis of variation is how the same rules become understandable and satisfying on a phone.

## A. Scorecard First

A compact conventional scorecard occupies most of the screen. Dice sit above it, and each available row shows a live score preview. Committed rows gain fabric color and texture until the list becomes a quilt-like strip.

- Persona fit: Most immediately legible to players who know the game.
- Design-challenge answer: Fastest route from opening the game to understanding the board.
- System fit: Smallest extension of Tumble's dice language.
- Best when: Familiarity and accessibility are more important than spectacle.
- Fails when: The finished artifact still reads as a decorated spreadsheet.
- Anti-pattern check: Passes; no dashboard, streak, public sharing, or productivity voice.

## B. Quilt Board

The scorecard becomes a two-column field of named patch tiles. Open tiles are quiet cloth outlines, rolled dice reveal live score previews, and a committed score fills its tile with a distinct fabric treatment. The dice remain directly above the board, so roll, pin, and stitch form one vertical reading path.

- Persona fit: Maya's group gets the familiar rules without losing the warm construction ritual.
- Design-challenge answer: Every turn visibly advances something the group can see taking shape.
- System fit: Reuses Games setup, dice feedback, buttons, and shelf grammar while adding one feature-owned board.
- Best when: The board remains readable on the smallest phone and does not need explanatory decoration.
- Fails when: Decorative patterns obscure category state or score previews.
- Anti-pattern check: Passes; the artifact is private by default and connection remains the job.

## C. Pattern Book

Only three recommended scoring choices are shown after each roll, with the full scorecard behind a “See every patch” action. The game teaches categories progressively and foregrounds the dice rather than the board.

- Persona fit: Helps true beginners start without reading thirteen categories.
- Design-challenge answer: Reduces the immediate choice set and teaches in play.
- System fit: Requires a recommendation policy and a reliable full-board escape hatch.
- Best when: New-player comprehension is the dominant problem.
- Fails when: Recommendations hide strategic options or make experienced players distrust the game.
- Anti-pattern check: Conditional pass; recommendations must be deterministic and transparent, not anthropomorphic AI.

## Recommendation

Choose **B. Quilt Board**. It most directly fulfills the user's stated metaphor without changing the rules. Borrow one property from Scorecard First: category names and score previews remain literal enough that experienced players never have to decode the theme.
