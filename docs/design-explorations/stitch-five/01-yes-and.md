# Yes-And: Stitch Five

## Decision

Skip expansion and keep the original frame bounded.

Stitch Five is intentionally a familiar five-dice scorecard game expressed through quilt-making. Its first job is not to invent a new strategy system; it is to make an already understandable game calmer, clearer, more tactile, and more satisfying to complete.

## Job elevation

The quilt metaphor can elevate `jtbd-help-us-enjoy-being-together` without adding rules:

- Pinned dice make the keep state unmistakable.
- Every legal category can preview its score before a player stitches.
- The quilt board can distinguish open, available, and completed patterns at a glance.
- Turn handoff can clearly name whose stitch comes next.
- The board can visibly assemble with every committed score and end as a finished quilt.
- A short first-game explanation can teach the immediate action instead of front-loading every category.

These are playability and presentation improvements, not claims of mechanical originality.

## Mechanics gate

Adjacency, region, color-family, symmetry, or free-placement bonuses remain workshop experiments. A mechanic advances only if comparison against the familiar rules shows that it:

1. creates a decision players understand without coaching;
2. does not materially lengthen a turn;
3. does not make score calculation harder to trust; and
4. increases voluntary replay or stated preference.

Otherwise, remove it.

## Frame recommendation

**Run the design-thinking loop with the original, bounded frame.** Divergence should compare presentation and playability approaches around the familiar rules, not invent unrelated game systems.
