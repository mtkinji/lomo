# Converge: Stitch Five

## Comparison

| Alternative | Familiarity | Finished-artifact value | Small-phone clarity | System fit | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scorecard First | Strongest | Weak | Strong | Strongest | Feels cosmetic |
| Quilt Board | Strong | Strongest | Good if reduced | Strong | Decoration can compete with state |
| Pattern Book | Moderate | Moderate | Strong | Moderate | Hides strategy |

## Chosen alternative

Build the Quilt Board with literal category labels and live score previews. The board is the scorecard; there is no second score view.

## Capability delta

Today, a group cannot choose a calm, constructive five-dice scorecard game from Kwilt's shelf.

After this release, two to four local players can seat themselves, roll five dice up to three times, pin dice between rolls, stitch one category per turn, see each player's quilt fill, finish after thirteen stitches each, and share a text rendering of the finished quilt.

Still intentionally unsupported: solo daily play, remote rooms, public results, streaks, spatial placement, extra five-of-a-kind bonuses, saved games, and image export.

## Rules contract

- Five dice; up to three rolls per stitch.
- A player may pin or unpin any die after the first roll.
- One unused category must be stitched after at least one roll; a legal zero is allowed.
- Six face categories score their matching dice.
- Three-piece and four-piece sets score the total of all dice.
- House Block scores 25, Short Stitch scores 30, Long Stitch scores 40, Free Patch scores the dice total, and Full Quilt scores 50.
- The face-pattern region earns a 35-point Seam Bonus at 63 points.
- Each player takes thirteen stitches; highest total wins and ties are allowed.

## Reductive design decisions

- One quilt board replaces separate category and progress views.
- No configurable rules, difficulty, tutorial carousel, score history, or account gate.
- Category language keeps the familiar rule in its supporting label where the quilt name alone could be ambiguous.
- Pin state uses position, border, text, and accessibility state—not color alone.
- The current turn has one dominant action: roll before dice exist; choose a patch after the third roll. Earlier score previews remain contextual actions.

## Activation

Stitch Five appears in the development Workshop with “Build a quilt, one roll at a time.” Setup reuses the existing optional-name flow. The first playable screen says “Roll all five,” then teaches pinning only after the first roll.

## Naming gate

“Stitch Five” replaces the conflicted Patchwork working title. It is concrete, easy to say at the table, and names both the action and the five-dice format. Production promotion still requires formal naming clearance.

## Bet

We're betting that familiar rules plus clearer pinning, live score previews, and visible construction will feel meaningfully better without additional scoring mechanics. If players describe it as slower or merely decorative, retain the interaction improvements and simplify the quilt treatment rather than adding rules.

## Success signal

Two first-time local players begin without coaching, finish a game with no scoring disputes, and voluntarily choose a rematch or share the finished quilt.
