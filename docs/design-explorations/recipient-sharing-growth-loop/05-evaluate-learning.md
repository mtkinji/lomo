# Evaluate Learning: Recipient Sharing Growth Loop

## Questions

1. Can the recipient explain what this invitation reveals and what remains private?
2. Can the recipient take one useful action without installing Kwilt?
3. Does the sender reliably receive and understand that action?
4. Does the recipient perceive account/app escalation as continuing value rather than paying an entrance fee?
5. Does either person initiate a second two-sided interaction within 14 days?
6. After repeated value, which paid depth feels natural to the organizer or participant?

## Evidence to collect

### Qualitative

- Ask recipients, before acting: “What do you think the sender will see if you continue?”
- Ask after acting: “What, if anything, would make installing Kwilt worthwhile now?”
- Ask senders: “Did this feel like support, a notification, or a referral?”
- At the second interaction: “Why did you choose this person or this channel again?”
- After two completed loops, show concepts—not a live paywall—for richer history, structured check-ins, reminders, more ongoing relationships, premium Games, and family administration.

### Behavioral

Collect aggregate, privacy-safe counts for:

- valid invitation landings;
- comprehension/continue action;
- guest cheer attempts and results;
- guest reply attempts and results;
- sender-visible delivery results;
- post-action continuation selections;
- context-preserving app opens;
- completed support loops;
- accounts reaching `support_loop_count` 0, 1, or 2-plus.

Do not collect invite codes, object IDs/titles, sender or recipient identities, friendship IDs, response content, message destinations, durable pair identifiers, or session replay.

## Small-cohort decision rules

Proceed beyond the learning cohort only if:

- zero privacy, authorization, attribution, or unintended-membership failures occur;
- every observed recipient correctly explains the Goal-only visibility boundary before acting;
- at least three two-person pairs complete recipient action → sender receipt without manual database intervention;
- at least two recipients take a meaningful web action;
- at least two senders open or otherwise acknowledge the received support; and
- at least one pair completes a second explicit interaction within 14 days.

These are learning thresholds, not long-term growth targets. They prove that the mechanism works well enough to instrument and scale.

## Interpretation

### If landing is strong but action is weak

- The invitation may be comprehensible but not personally relevant.
- Improve the meaningful context or response affordance before adding stronger install prompts.
- Do not solve this with urgency, badges, or referral rewards.

### If action is strong but sender receipt is weak

- Activation is not complete.
- Fix delivery, feed visibility, or sender notification before scaling acquisition.

### If the first loop works but repeat is weak

- Test whether continuing follow, Friends, or a new share supplies a clearer next job.
- Do not assume more notifications will create durable value.

### If repeat is strong but monetization concepts are weak

- Preserve the free support loop.
- Investigate whether the payer is the organizer, the family administrator, or a participant seeking richer personal history.
- Do not gate the recipient's basic response.

### If privacy comprehension is weak

- Stop expansion.
- Simplify the envelope, strengthen object-scoped copy, and make ending/revocation more visible.

## Next capability gate

Only after the Goal loop passes may the same envelope expand:

1. **Friend:** post-value relationship acceptance and reusable recipient selection.
2. **Games:** bounded guest participation with explicit save/identity escalation.
3. **Household and Screen Time:** preview only on the web; verified app/native authority remains mandatory.

Each capability must define its own visibility, consent, lifecycle, abuse, and terminal-state tests before using the shared shell.
