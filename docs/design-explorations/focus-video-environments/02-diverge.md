# Diverge: Focus video environments

## Evidence posture

Portal is a useful behavioral reference, not a source to copy. Its current App Store presentation combines full-screen motion visuals, immersive location audio, and a simple focus timer around the promise of feeling present in a natural place. Its 4.8 rating across roughly 10,000 ratings demonstrates demand and product acceptance, but not which individual scene causes better focus or whether the product improves attention. Andrew's repeated use of Portal's Bluebell Woods demonstrates the value of feeling enclosed by a place; his preference for Utah desert landscapes and desire for a more distinctive Kwilt scene now point the first learning release toward an original slot canyon.

The research is mixed and does not justify a medical or productivity claim:

- A 2023 preregistered within-subject experiment found no evidence that nature videos restored executive attention relative to urban videos.
- A 2025 forest-soundscape trial reported better mood, perceived restoration, and cognitive outcomes than industrial soundscapes, but not improvement across all physiological measures.
- Audiovisual waterscape and multisensory studies suggest that congruent natural sound and image can improve perceived or physiological restoration, but they do not establish which Focus backdrop will help this user complete more meaningful work.

## Axis of variation

Personally demonstrated affinity versus audiovisual research fit versus perfect-loop feasibility.

## Alternative A: Bluebell Clearing

Create an original spring woodland: tall, stable deciduous trunks, a broad carpet of bluebells, soft overcast daylight, and only minute leaf and flower movement. The scene borrows the job shape Andrew valued in Portal's Bluebell Woods—quiet enclosure and a sense of being somewhere else—without copying Portal's footage, composition, audio, or product naming.

- Persona fit: strongest; Andrew has already spent meaningful time in a bluebell woodland environment.
- Design-challenge answer: makes Focus feel like entering one sheltered place rather than starting a timer over a decorative background.
- System fit: one new video-backed environment inside the existing Focus selection; muted video and separately mastered woodland audio.
- Best when: personal affinity and gentle enclosure matter more than broad scenic drama.
- Fails when: foliage, dappled light, focus breathing, or bird events reveal the loop.
- Four-object model: enhances Focus around an Activity or standalone session; creates no new object.
- Capture-first: unchanged; Focus and Activity capture remain available without this environment.
- Anti-pattern check: pass if the scene stays quiet and optional; fail if it becomes a collectible media library.

## Alternative B: Quiet Shore

Create an original calm beach seen from a fixed seated viewpoint, with a wide horizon, small waves, no people, boats, gull calls, or dramatic weather. The water and surf provide congruent audiovisual motion that some multisensory research associates with restoration.

- Persona fit: good; Andrew already likes a Portal beach scene.
- Design-challenge answer: open horizon and steady surf can make the phone feel like a window rather than an interface.
- System fit: same one-environment model as Bluebell Clearing.
- Best when: water rhythm feels settling and masks household noise.
- Fails when: wave sets expose a reset, the horizon shifts by a fraction, or surf becomes attention-grabbing.
- Four-object model: enhances Focus only.
- Capture-first: unchanged.
- Anti-pattern check: pass, but the visual loop is materially harder to make unnoticeable than a low-motion woodland.

## Alternative C: Canyon Spring

Create an original Utah-inspired slot-canyon chamber: stable red sandstone walls and a small natural arch frame a shallow spring, a thin continuous waterfall, and sparse hanging-garden plants. The camera and indirect light remain locked. Only water texture and low-amplitude plant movement change. The base audio is soft air moving through stone with continuous water; distinct drips and birds are omitted because recognizable events expose repetition.

- Persona fit: strongest current direction; it combines Andrew's affinity for Utah desert landscapes with the sheltered, inhabitable quality he valued in Bluebell Woods.
- Design-challenge answer: the canyon feels like a place around the work, while stable rock geometry keeps the scene calm enough to recede.
- System fit: the static canyon is loop-friendly, but the water requires stricter motion acceptance than a dry overlook.
- Best when: originality, enclosure, and a clear Kwilt identity matter more than copying a proven scenic category.
- Fails when: water volume pulses, reflections jump, plants sway out of phase, or generated geology becomes visually implausible.
- Four-object model: enhances Focus only.
- Capture-first: unchanged.
- Anti-pattern check: pass if it remains one quiet chamber; fail if arches, waterfalls, plants, and wildlife become a scenic highlight reel.

## Recommendation

Converge on **Canyon Spring** for the first learning release. Bluebell Clearing has the strongest prior-use evidence, but Canyon Spring better combines Andrew's underlying preference for sheltered immersion with a landscape that can feel distinctly Kwilt. Its stable sandstone geometry should make camera drift easy to detect, while the thin waterfall and shallow stream provide enough continuous motion and sound to make the place feel alive. If the generated water cannot meet the direct-cut loop bar, simplify the same scene to a still spring and gentle plant movement rather than changing environments.
