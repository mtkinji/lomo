# Evaluate Learning: Canyon Spring

## Learning questions

- Does a coordinated landscape make Focus feel easier to enter than the current flat color with audio?
- Does Andrew settle into the scene, or watch and inspect it?
- Is the visual still valuable after novelty fades?
- Can the direct-cut video and multi-minute audio run through ordinary sessions without a noticed boundary?
- Does portrait feel intentionally framed rather than like a crop of landscape?
- Is keeping the screen visible worth the battery and thermal cost?

## Evidence plan

Use six ordinary sessions rather than a staged attention test:

- Three sessions with Canyon Spring.
- Three comparable sessions with the same duration using an audio-only environment.
- Alternate conditions rather than completing all sessions of one kind first.

After each session, record only:

- Whether a video or audio loop was noticed and approximately when.
- Whether the scene drew attention away from the work.
- Whether the phone stayed visible, was turned face-down, or was locked.
- Whether the environment would be chosen again for the next similar session.
- A one-sentence description of how starting and settling felt.

Supporting technical evidence:

- First-frame playback time from uncached and cached state.
- Fallback frequency.
- Battery percentage and thermal-state observation across one 25-minute portrait session and one landscape session.
- Background/lock-screen audio continuity and clean session end.

## Disconfirming signals

- Any repeat boundary is noticed during ordinary work.
- The scene is watched, inspected, or treated as content.
- The user consistently locks or turns over the phone immediately.
- Portrait composition feels compromised.
- Video loading delays Focus, produces heat, or makes fallback noisy.
- The scene is chosen once for novelty and then abandoned.

## Instrumentation

Allow local diagnostic events for environment selected, video ready, video fallback, orientation, and playback error. Do not record gaze, detailed listening behavior, scene-specific minute counts, or inferred attention. Manual dogfood notes are the primary learning record.

## Decision rule

- Proceed: at least two of three scene sessions are voluntarily followed by another desire to use the scene, no loop is noticed, and the phone remains visible for a meaningful part of the work without thermal concern.
- Revise: immersion is valued but the loop, portrait framing, or power behavior fails. Replace the asset or simplify to poster-plus-audio before adding another scene.
- Retire: the visual repeatedly distracts, the phone is consistently hidden, or audio-only Focus is preferred after novelty.

## Expected next action

If the bet holds, test a second composition from the same desert language—an open arch or high-desert overlook—before expanding into unrelated scenic categories. Do not build a broad library until two distinct scenes show repeat use.
