# Evaluate Learning: Audio in context

## Learning questions

- Does remote-first playback feel immediate on normal Wi-Fi and cellular service?
- Does a repeated session use the cached source without behavioral differences?
- Do the Focus and game candidates improve the actual moment rather than merely sound good alone?
- Are instructions and conversation effortless over game music?
- Do any cues cause an instinctive device-volume adjustment?
- Does fallback behavior preserve trust when the selected track is uncached and unavailable?

## Evidence

Supporting evidence:

- No visible wait or abandoned start during normal connected use.
- Cache-source diagnostics on second play.
- Andrew, Olive, and Charlie can describe where music helps or distracts in actual play.
- Fixed-volume comparisons remain comfortable.

Disconfirming evidence:

- Audible startup gaps, buffering, doubled playback, failure to stop, or background-route regressions.
- Family members ask to mute because music masks talk or feels childish/news-like.
- The temporary Bank transitions feel less coherent than silence.
- Cellular transfer duplication is materially wasteful in practice.

## Instrumentation

Record only technical events: track id, source kind (`bundled`, `cache`, `remote`, `fallback`), prepare latency, download success/failure, playback error, and cache bytes. Do not record listening content, nearby speech, player reactions, or inferred taste.

## Decision rule

After Andrew completes at least three Focus sessions and one representative round in each integrated game:

- Keep and refine candidates that improve the moment without reliability or volume problems.
- Replace or remove candidates that distract, mask speech, or feel generic.
- Revisit delivery only if startup, duplicated transfer, or offline behavior becomes noticeable.
