# Diverge: Family Screen Time Prerequisite Activity

Axis of variation: what counts as trustworthy evidence that the family intention came first?

## A. Required App Threshold

The caregiver selects a prerequisite app and a target selection. Device Activity counts only foreground use of the prerequisite and unlocks the target when the threshold fires.

- Persona fit: strong; the ordinary transition happens without caregiver action.
- Design challenge: directly answers the requested agreement while retaining Apple's privacy boundary.
- System fit: extends the existing rule with `prerequisiteActivity`; Activities and the four-object model remain unchanged.
- Best when: the intended action lives in a specific app.
- Fails when: app use is treated as proof of content or intent.
- Anti-pattern check: pass with explicit “use for five minutes” copy.

## B. Kwilt Activity Completion

Chat creates or references a recurring “Read scripture” Activity, and Games unlock after Charlie marks the occurrence complete.

- Persona fit: medium; it works across physical and offline activities.
- Design challenge: supports the intention but does not satisfy the requested foreground-time evidence.
- System fit: uses the four-object model directly but depends on mature household Activity assignment.
- Best when: the responsibility has no measurable app surface.
- Fails when: a one-tap completion becomes a weak proxy for the requested five minutes.
- Anti-pattern check: pass, but it is a different rule.

## C. Scheduled Delay

Games stay shielded until a chosen time; the family treats the earlier period as scripture time without measuring Gospel Library.

- Persona fit: medium; setup is simple and private.
- Design challenge: creates rhythm but cannot express “at least five minutes.”
- System fit: already supported conceptually by rule windows.
- Best when: the family cares about sequence more than evidence.
- Fails when: Charlie skips Gospel Library and Games still open.
- Anti-pattern check: pass, but it does not fulfill the offered idea.

## D. Child Confirmation

Charlie taps “I did it” in Kwilt to unlock Games, with the agreement relying on trust rather than device measurement.

- Persona fit: medium; it preserves agency and works beyond app use.
- Design challenge: keeps the child legible but adds a manual ceremony.
- System fit: resembles Activity completion but should not create a parallel check-in object.
- Best when: foreground usage is unavailable.
- Fails when: the family specifically asked the device to carry the timing burden.
- Anti-pattern check: risk of forced commitment; keep only as a future fallback.
