## Player win signatures

### Purpose and playback context

Power chord and the related candidates were intended as **player-selected success signatures**. A person attaches one to their profile, and Kwilt plays it when that player owns a meaningful success or win. They are not Bank mechanics, dice sounds, or generic button acknowledgements.

The listening room did not make that ownership and playback contract clear enough. Future reviews should pair the cue with the player's identity, the win animation, the fading game score, and the actual duration of the outcome moment.

### Direction correction

The current candidates validate several sound identities, but not their musical shape. A player-selected win signature should occupy enough time to make the outcome feel celebrated without delaying the next action.

- **Power chord → Power lick:** a bright clean-guitar attack followed by a compact, playful ascending lick and confident resolved note. It should feel performed, not like one sampled chord.
- **Banjo note → Banjo victory run:** a crisp pickup into a lively, skillful phrase with a small lift or flourish and a clearly resolved final strum. It should feel like a miniature performance rather than a pluck, sting, or generic country cliché.
- **Drum triumph:** a short fill plus a conclusive ensemble cadence, rather than one impact.
- **Magic portal:** a readable rise, opening shimmer, and warm resolved landing.
- **Tiny crowd:** a quick anticipatory swell into a compact cheer, with no intelligible words.
- Apply the same review question to every other win candidate: does it tell a tiny celebratory story, or merely announce that something happened?

### Production contract to test

- Target duration: **1.5–2.5 seconds** for player-selected wins.
- Preserve the existing `game.signature` loudness target and cross-candidate balance.
- Favor attack → flourish → resolution; avoid long reverb tails that only create nominal duration.
- Keep the cue interruptible and allow play to continue underneath it.
- Regenerate the promising families before adding more unrelated win concepts.

### Vibe-based signature library

The library should help people choose a sound that feels like *their* kind of celebration or comic setback. Organize generation and review around recognizable identity lanes rather than an undifferentiated list of instruments and effects.

Potential lanes to explore:

| Vibe | Success direction | Setback direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rocker | Glorious power lick, compact drum-and-guitar finish | Amp sputter, pick scrape into a comic unresolved chord |
| Hip-hop | Original beat-drop flourish, confident drum/bass tag | Beat dropout, tape stop, playful sub-bass deflation |
| Internet creator | Original high-energy reveal burst or enthusiastic creator-style sting | Original anticlimax, glitch-out, or comedic momentum loss |
| Nature / animal | Hawk cry, wolf howl, majestic but safe animal call | Surprised chicken, goat reaction, harmless creature interruption |
| Fantasy | Portal opens and resolves, spell success, enchanted landing | Spell fizzles, tiny magical collapse, failed incantation texture |
| Sports / crowd | Compact stadium lift, buzzer-to-cheer gesture | Crowd “ooh” texture without words, comic missed-shot gesture |
| Handmade / acoustic | Banjo run, hand-percussion flourish | String stumble, wooden wobble, deflating folk cadence |

These are creative territories, not stereotypes assigned to children. Every player may choose from the entire library.

### Originality and rights boundary

Andrew cited the emotional function of a recognizable film character saying “Yes!” and familiar YouTube-style “awesome” sounds. Use those only as evidence that a short, culturally legible vocal or creator-style gesture can carry success on its own.

- Do not use copyrighted film clips, recognizable dialogue recordings, branded creator stings, or celebrity/character voice imitation.
- An original human exclamation may be explored if it uses an original phrase and voice with clear commercial-use provenance.
- Internet-native cues should capture energy and timing without copying a specific channel, meme sound, or platform asset.

### Outcome weight

- Most success signatures should run **1.5–2.5 seconds** and feel complete on their own.
- A naturally meaningful sound such as a hawk cry may work without added music because its full gesture already carries weight and cultural meaning.
- Setback signatures may remain shorter, normally **1.0–1.8 seconds**, but still need a readable setup and comic resolution rather than a single arbitrary noise.
- The game score should duck or stop so the selected profile sound owns the outcome.

### Wolf howl refinement

**Status:** Keep the concept; reject the current executions.

The next Wolf howl candidates should sound like a real adult wolf recorded outdoors, not a synthetic animal approximation, processed human voice, cartoon wolf, or domestic dog.

- Preserve the breath-led onset, chest resonance, complex pitch movement, and slight natural roughness of a real howl.
- Give the call enough duration and dynamic shape to feel powerful as a success signature.
- Use believable outdoor reflections and distance; avoid artificial cavern reverb or cinematic horror treatment.
- Aim for triumphant, wild intensity rather than aggression, threat, or fear.
- A faint distant pack response may be tested as a variant, but the primary call must remain clear on an iPhone speaker.
- Reject takes with static single-note synthesis, obvious looping, canine whining, or exaggerated monster bass.

Acceptance check: without seeing its label, listeners should identify it as a real wolf and experience the call as a confident win gesture.

### Tiny Crowd refinement

**Status:** Keep the concept; reject the current entrances and duration.

The current candidates feel clipped at the front, as though recording began partway through an existing celebration. A player win needs to cause the cheer audibly.

- Begin with a clean triggering moment: a tiny anticipatory intake, one or two initial voices, or a very brief rise from silence.
- Build rapidly into the full miniature crowd rather than entering at peak density.
- Include a readable celebration peak and natural decay or resolved final gesture.
- Target approximately **2.0–2.5 seconds**, subject to contextual testing against the win animation.
- Keep the crowd whimsical and compact, not like a huge stadium or canned television audience.
- Use no intelligible words, names, chants, or borrowed recordings.
- Preserve the `game.signature` loudness target; do not use a louder master to create excitement.

Acceptance check: the cue should sound like the player's win caused a complete crowd reaction, not like Kwilt faded into the middle of another recording.

**Status:** Near-keeper; preserve the musical composition and refine the rain layer.

Andrew loves Rainlit Library 1 except for one weather-texture quirk: the rain repeatedly grows louder and softer in a slow swell. That modulation reads like waves arriving on a shore rather than natural, continuous rainfall.

Refinement direction:

- Preserve Rainlit Library 1's music, pacing, restrained melody, and overall mix character.
- Replace the pulsing or breathing rain envelope with a stable natural rain bed.
- Avoid periodic swells, surf-like whooshes, shoreline cadence, and obvious looping cycles.
- Use consistent rainfall energy with small irregular droplet variation, not large changes in overall rain loudness.
- Keep the rain behind the music and at a stable perceived level across the full four minutes.
- Prefer a reference-based variation or targeted edit. Do not attempt broad full-mix compression as a substitute for correcting the rain texture, because that could flatten the music while leaving the surf-like pattern audible.

Acceptance check: listen for at least 60 seconds without the rain suggesting a repeating inhale/exhale or waves approaching and receding.

## Bank adaptive music

### Current full-mix mapping hypothesis

This mapping applies only if Kwilt uses the present standalone candidate tracks as an MVP. It is not the preferred final architecture.

| Bank state | Candidate | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial at-risk state | Pot is growing | Preferred mapping |
| Building risk | High stakes | Open question; evaluate between the other two tracks in context |
| Highest-risk state | First risky rolls | Preferred mapping |

Do not use the candidate filenames or original review labels as evidence of intensity. The state mapping should follow perceived tension in family play.

### Preferred final architecture: one composition with vertical layers

Andrew's preferred Bank experience is one continuous underlying track that gains additional musical layers as the risk rises. The listener should feel the same piece becoming more intense, not hear Kwilt switch between three separate songs.

Use phase-locked stems with identical tempo, key, duration, bar structure, and loop boundaries:

1. **Base stem:** the stable musical identity of the round; sparse enough to leave room for later layers.
2. **Low-risk layer:** a restrained pulse or light rhythmic detail entering at the first at-risk state.
3. **Building layer:** denser percussion, bass movement, or another complementary texture after additional safe rolls.
4. **Maximum-risk layer:** the fullest rhythmic or harmonic-tension material, still playful and never simply louder.

All stems should begin on the same timeline and remain phase aligned. Inactive stems can run silently or be started at the matching bar position. Bring layers in over one or two bars after the safe-roll outcome, so the change feels natural and almost inevitable rather than like a sound effect was switched on.

Adding stems must not create a large loudness jump. Master and mix every combined tier as a unit; as density increases, compensate the bus or rebalance the base so each tier remains within its target range.

This approach also defines the downward transitions:

- **Bank the pot:** let the lock-in outcome cue resolve, then strip the added layers away or stop the score as the round resets.
- **Seven out:** duck the layered score beneath the release/collapse cue, then clear the layers for the next round.
- **Game end:** stop the adaptive score so the player's longer win signature owns the moment.

### Bank-the-pot outcome cue

Reject the current **lock-in** candidates. A literal lock, latch, click, or abstract mechanical confirmation is the wrong analogy: the emotional action is not closing a door, but collecting value before it can be lost.

The replacement should use a compact coin gesture:

- Begin with a few distinct coins gathering or jingling so the material reads immediately.
- Resolve with the coins settling securely into a small container, stack, or hand—not a loose spill.
- Feel rewarding and prudent rather than jackpot-sized; banking is a satisfying choice, not necessarily winning the game.
- Avoid cash-register bells, slot-machine language, casino fanfare, or a harsh metallic pileup.
- Keep it short enough to support the next action, approximately 0.8–1.3 seconds, with a complete onset and decay.
- Mix it to the Bank outcome-effect target and audition it over every adaptive-music tier; clarity should come from spectral space and a brief music duck, not a louder master.

Acceptance check: without seeing the button, a listener should plausibly describe the moment as “the coins were collected” or “the pot was secured,” rather than “something locked.”

### Doubles outcome cue

Reject the current **doubles hit** candidates. A generic impact does not express what happened: doubles are a surprising, positive mid-round event that deserves a brief reaction.

Test two directions in gameplay:

1. **Tiny Crowd — compact variant:** a complete but abbreviated crowd swell and cheer, approximately 1.0–1.5 seconds. It may share the Tiny Crowd identity, but should be smaller than the 2.0–2.5 second player-win signature.
2. **Bank-specific success flourish:** a short original upward rhythmic or musical gesture that feels lucky and playful without becoming a jackpot sting.

The doubles cue should briefly duck the adaptive score, land after the dice result becomes legible, and allow the music to continue naturally. It must not be confused with banking the pot, winning the game, or triggering the player's profile victory signature. Audition repeated doubles as well as a single occurrence; the sound should remain fun without becoming exhausting.

### Fallback transition hypothesis for the current full mixes

- Change tiers only after a safe roll has resolved and the pot/risk state has visibly updated; never switch while the die is moving.
- Let the roll or Bank mechanical cue briefly mask the transition.
- Duck the outgoing music under that cue, then use an equal-power crossfade into the incoming track.
- Start by testing a 1.0–1.2 second crossfade. If the tracks' beats clash, move the transition to the next bar boundary or use a short fade-down / downbeat-start rather than lengthening an incoherent overlap.
- A tier change should feel caused by the growing pot, not like an unrelated song ended.
- Do not increase runtime gain between tiers. Perceived intensity must come from the selected arrangement.

2. High stakes after the next qualifying safe roll.
3. First risky rolls at the maximum-risk threshold.
4. Bank the pot, then repeat with a seven-out ending.

Record whether High stakes works as the bridge, whether any transition causes an instinctive volume adjustment, and whether the mechanical cue adequately hides the musical handoff.

## Story and connection-game soundtracks

### Current assessment

The current candidates are fun and already improve the games relative to having no music. They are not yet final-quality Kwilt identities. Across the set, bright explanatory gestures, familiar playful instrumentation, and consistently active arrangements create a shared “children's program” or “news report” quality.

The next iteration should not merely produce better generic background music. Each game should establish a recognizable sonic world.

### Reference principles

Andrew cited *The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild* and *Tears of the Kingdom* as an exceptionally strong example of a unique game soundtrack, and Gran Turismo's use of licensed popular music as another memorable soundtrack experience.

Use those references only for transferable principles, never for imitation:

- From the Zelda reference: restraint, meaningful silence, sparse motifs, environmental integration, unusual but coherent timbres, and music that responds to the experience rather than continuously narrating it.
- From the Gran Turismo reference: confident curation, contemporary taste, and music that gives the experience a mature cultural identity rather than sounding like stock “game music.” Do not imitate or generate soundalikes of licensed songs.

### Direction correction

- Reduce bright, literal “playful” signifiers such as constant pizzicato, marimba punctuation, jaunty brass answers, and presenter-like cadences.
- Avoid wall-to-wall melody. Let texture, rhythm, silence, and a few recognizable motifs carry identity.
- Give each game a constrained material palette that could be recognized without seeing the screen.
- Prefer mature curiosity and wonder over child-coded whimsy; Kwilt can be legible to children without sounding made only for children.
- Design music around phase and interaction: composing, searching, waiting, guessing, revealing, and handing off may need different density or silence.
- Treat “no continuous score” or a sparse environmental soundscape as a valid result when it gives a game more identity.

### Game-specific questions for the next pass

- **Story Relay:** What is the sound of a story being passed between people, rather than generic storybook adventure?
- **Doodle Bridge:** Can mark-making, paper, graphite, and connection become rhythmic material without turning into novelty Foley?
- **Object Quest:** Can the room itself feel newly explorable through a restrained field-recording or expedition palette?
- **Clue Circle:** What makes its social deduction feel intimate and clever rather than like television mystery music?
- **Slanguage:** Can it have a confident clubhouse or subculture identity without sounding like children's educational programming?

Keep the present tracks available as the MVP comparison. The refinement succeeds only if listeners can identify which game a track belongs to before reading its label.
